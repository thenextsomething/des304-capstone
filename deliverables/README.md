## The four workshops

All four run the same way: a compulsory lecture plus workshop in class, fill out the template provided, reflect on how the taught concepts apply to your own project, upload the completed template as a PDF by the end of that day. 2.5 marks each, 10 marks total, graded on whether the reflection is genuinely applied to your project rather than generic.

They are cheap marks but they work like attendance: you cannot make them up later if you miss the class.

## The A3 cluster is a crunch

Four deliverables land inside 15 days:

- **15 Oct** posters (and late here is punished hard, because they have to be printed for the exhibition)
- **21 Oct** oral presentation
- **22 Oct at 9am** visual project report, which is the morning after the presentation and is worth 40 marks on its own
- **29 Oct** prototype

Two things follow from the ordering. First, the posters need finished hero shots of the project in use two full weeks before the prototype is even due, so the visual material cannot wait for the prototype deadline. Second, the report and the presentation are effectively one push, so the report cannot be left to start after the presentation is done.

## The three requirements that press hardest on the Compass project

These are the places where the course brief and the project's current plan actually collide. Detail is in each deliverable file.

1. **A physical prototype is compulsory, even though the deliverable is a film.** The brief says explicitly that a physical prototype is expected even when the outcome is intangible, and that a high-fidelity version must be shown live at the final presentation and the exhibition. For Compass this means the glasses as an object are not an optional side quest, they are 15 marks. The exact format is listed as "TBC with your stream coordinator", so this needs a direct conversation with the stream leader about whether the film is the prototype, the glasses are the prototype, or both.

2. **No AI-generated images are allowed in the posters.** Royalty-free stock is allowed for hero shots if captioned and cited on the poster itself, with the full citation in the report. This rules out using generated storyboard or concept frames as the poster hero shots, so the hero shots have to come from real footage or real photographs of the physical prototype.

3. **The report must follow the chosen design methodology, and it must be a curated printed book.** The brief names Discover, Define, Develop and Deliver as the example structure, which is the double diamond that the lecturers approved on 4 August. The brief also states there is no evaluation of the Miro board itself, so all the process material on the board has to be pulled out and designed into the report. The board is the working space, not the submission.

## Course structure

- 12-week capstone paper, roughly 30 hours per week, near full-time commitment.
- Tuesdays 10am to 1pm: all streams together. The only lecture of the week (1 to 1.5 hours, then studio time). Guest lectures from all stream leaders across the semester.
- Thursdays: split by stream, full day (10am to 1pm, then 2pm to 4pm). Mornings are stream leader check-ins, one-on-ones and workshopping; afternoons are GTA-led studio stand-ups.
- Technology + Aging streams work in the Michael Gander Studio at Elam. Aldo is based there with digital prototyping expertise; extra equipment can be negotiated.
- Empathic computing lab resources available to relevant projects.
- 9 timetabled studio hours; the rest is self-directed. The 12-week plan lives on Canvas.

## Assignments

- **A1 (15 pts):** concept pitch to partners, 10 pts, **due next Thursday morning (30 July)**; Positionality Workshop 2.5 pts; Speculative Prototyping Workshop 2.5 pts.
- **A2:** compulsory post-break check-in plus two workshops (impact planning, material choices).
- **A3:** final submissions, staggered. Posters first (printed ahead of the exhibition), then the visual and oral presentation for partners.
- Workshops are assessed and feed directly into the final report.

## The A1 concept pitch (due Thursday 30 July)

- **One idea only**, at concept level. 3-minute hard limit, cut off strictly for equity. No scripts, no reading off slides (notes on screen are fine).
- Slides highly visual. No Canva decorations; intentional branding or a simple palette.
- Must cover: a research-backed why (problem plus evidence), a refined HMW question, the concept proposal with a visual (drawing, AI-generated, collage), stakeholders and why they would participate, and a basic explanation of how it works (storyboard or simple system map).
- Submit: slide deck PDF, project management plan PDF, and a Miro board link accessible to markers.

## Project management plan

- 12-week timeline printout submitted alongside the Miro board link.
- Use a Miro educational account (free); all digital materials should live on Miro for check-ins.
- Timeline includes: weekly milestones and tasks, design methodology mapped to stages, methods and tools, updated research from 300-level, and reflection on cultural positionality.
- Purpose is a scope check: is the project too big or too small.

## Academic integrity and AI use

- All assignments are Lane 2 (uncontrolled): **AI is permitted but must be acknowledged**. Cite AI like any other source: tool name, prompt used, date, reference list entry. Provide a summary of how AI was used, not full chat logs. Applies to ideation, writing review, image generation and code.
- Plagiarism or undisclosed AI use risks failing the course.
- **Primary research with human subjects is not permitted** at undergraduate level. Allowed: talking to partners, informal chats with family and friends, field trip conversations with consent. Not allowed: formal interviews or testing with the public. No photographing people without consent; no identifiable faces from the internet.
- Cultural appropriation is a red flag: avoid AI depictions of Maori symbols or moko unless confident in the context.
- Use AI for tedious tasks; do not let it lead design decisions. Polished AI diagrams that lack meaning will be failed. Make your own diagram first, then refine aesthetics with AI if needed.

## Graduate show

- Week 14, curated: not all projects exhibit, quality bar applies, integrity breaches excluded. All students attend and help set up.

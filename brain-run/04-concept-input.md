## A. The core

- **The big idea, unpacked.** Every product in this category fills your vision
  the second you put it on. Compass does not. The film's whole argument is a
  thing that is not there, and the only way to show a thing that is not there
  is to hold on it long enough that the audience notices the absence. So the
  film opens on nothing, three times, and each time a person starts and the
  lens catches up a beat later.

  This is the one truth FP-23 demands, and it is already written in the
  product definition: **"The empty middle state is the feature, not the
  absence of one."** Every segment has to prove it, sharpen it or pay it off.
  A segment that only decorates it is cut, however well it is made.

- **The villain, confirmed** - **the replacement default**: the assumption,
  built into the products actually being shipped, that the useful thing for a
  machine to do is make the thing for you (profile block 2, FP-14).

  **Confirmed with a named divergence.** FP-14 says the villain is the spine
  and not a seasoning, and FP-21 says the viewer must feel the imbalance before
  the resolution means anything. The ruled register forbids both: no struggle,
  no failure beat. So this film **has a villain that never appears on screen**,
  which is a genuine break with two active brain rules.

  The reason to hold the divergence rather than fix it is the project's own
  precedent finding: "if the film read as an obvious critique from the first
  frame it would stop being a diegetic prototype and start being an essay". A
  dramatised villain would win FP-21 and lose the whole method.

  **What carries the villain instead, and this is the concept decision:** the
  villain lives in **what Compass refuses to do**, made visible as an absence.
  The empty lens is the negative dosed like salt. It is the film saying, once,
  without argument, that there is a hole here exactly where every competitor
  would have put something. That is FP-21 honoured at the level of
  design rather than drama, and it is the only reading under which the
  no-struggle ruling and the villain rule can both survive. **Needs Polo's
  ruling** (section G, item 2).

- **The one feeling, confirmed** - **"I made that, and I wasn't on my own."**
  Ownership without loneliness. Compounded from two lines already in the
  record: "not by replacing the maker, but by changing what it feels like to
  make" and "you never have to be alone in the process if you do not want
  to be".

- **The tell-a-friend moment (FP-13)** - **the guide lines that vanish the
  instant the pencil stops.** It is the only moment where the product's whole
  argument happens in under a second with nobody saying anything, and it is
  retellable in one sentence: the glasses stop helping the second you stop.
  The board already ranks the same beat itself.

## B. The open

- **Sit-down or hook?** Hook, and third person, which the record already ruled
  ("The opening scenes are third person, to introduce the characters and to
  show them putting the glasses on, then it moves inside their view").

- **The specific opening image.** **A person puts the glasses on, and nothing
  happens.** Held. Held past the point where the audience expects the interface
  to arrive, which in this category is immediately.

  This passes FP-04's pattern-interrupt test in the strongest way available:
  the norm in the category is that the lens fills the moment the device goes on,
  and the project's own precedent analysis has already established that every
  sell film in the set shows the device working and nothing in it ever fails.
  Opening on a device doing nothing is the only move in the set that nobody has
  made.

  It also has a named precedent in the brain, and it is an Apple one, which
  matches the ruled register exactly: the `openai-apple` reference set's take
  from "Great Ideas Start On Mac" (CV-0037) is **"hold on literal nothing and
  let it earn the payoff"** - one unbroken 42-second push-in on a blank laptop
  screen before the frame erupts. That is this open, at a quarter of the
  length.

  VG-AWARE-04 rules out the alternative: the category slot exists and is
  crowded, so the bare claim is not available and the open has to
  differentiate. "Here is a new pair of AI glasses" is dead on arrival.

- **The before rule.** Minimal to zero. The viewer is `problem-aware` and
  already living the before (profile block 1, VG-AWARE-03). The film does not
  explain the pain and does not dramatise it. The statistics passage the record
  already plans is the only place the before appears at all, and it is
  information rather than drama.

- **CP-LAUNCH-05, and the one place this film has to be careful.** The base
  concept has to land in the first seconds: the viewer has to know what they
  are signing up for even behind a hold-on-nothing open. **Here that is
  answered by the object, not by a line** - the glasses go on in frame one, so
  the category is legible before the lens is. That is the only thing letting
  the open hold for as long as it needs to.

- **CP-LAUNCH-07, the first-guess test.** What does a cold viewer guess this
  is after the first beat? The honest answer today is "AI glasses", and the
  honest risk is that they then guess "a heads-up display" - which is the
  category Compass is most at risk of being mistaken for, and the two arguments
  that used to separate it from one both died on 16 September (truth sheet A2
  and A8). **So the reveal has to do that work**, and the film cannot rely on
  restraint in rendering to do it. It has to be restraint in timing, shown.

## C. The body

- **The scene premise, one sentence.** Three people in three rooms begin work,
  and the lens wakes for each of them at the moment their hands do.

- **The device.** **The wake.** The thing that binds three people who never
  meet is the same event happening three times at three crafts: the first key,
  the first frame, the first mark, and the lens arriving a beat behind each
  one. The intercut is not three stories running in parallel, it is one moment
  told three ways.

  This is the device that makes the film legible without a narrator, and it is
  what lets dialogue stay where the record put it - actors talking about their
  day, not about the product.

- **The hero product moments, ranked**, drawn from profile block 4 with their
  truth status carried:

| # | Moment | Truth status | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The empty lens, on, showing nothing | `[shipped]` | the big idea; cheapest shot in the film |
| 2 | Guide lines that go the moment the pencil stops | `[shipped]`, `[UNCONFIRMED: A3]` `[UNCONFIRMED: A6]` | the tell-a-friend moment |
| 3 | The falling-note lane and the key lighting at the moment to press | `[shipped]` | **the hardest composite in the film** - the only interface sitting on a real object |
| 4 | Four panels around an empty centre | `[shipped]` 16 Sep, `[UNCONFIRMED: A1]` `[UNCONFIRMED: A5]` | proves a busy lens can still leave the centre clear |
| 5 | The reference layer in full colour | `[shipped]`, `[UNCONFIRMED: A1]` | the one colour exception |
| 6 | Everything on the device, nothing kept | `[shipped]` | **has no shot and is not getting one** - see the placement note below |

  **The count, against CP-LAUNCH-01.** The rule allows 3 to 4 hero moments
  inside the arc, each one real. Moments 1 to 4 are the four. Moment 5 is not a
  fifth moment, it is a property of moment 4's panel set and it appears inside
  the same shot. Moment 6 leaves the film entirely, below. So the film carries
  four, which is the rule's ceiling and not an exception to it.

  **Cut from the set, deliberately:** the proportion corrections (until truth
  row A4 is ruled, they read as a machine marking your work wrong, which is the
  one thing this film cannot show), and the light reading (truth row A9; it
  folds into settings and stops being a moment).

  **Where moment 6 goes instead.** It is the project's second real
  differentiator and it carries the Te Tiriti section of the report, and it is
  a claim with no image. Forcing it into the film would cost a segment and buy
  a title card. **Decision: it lives in the press kit, the landing page and the
  report, not in the film.** RS-CORE-03 is the rule behind that - held is not
  used, and an asset argued into a video it does not fit is how a kitchen-sink
  video starts.

- **The compositing budget, and this is the HAVE fork's real bite.**
  RS-PRODUCT-05 says to cap the product's screen time to the material's depth
  when the material is thin. **Here the material is not thin, it is
  non-existent: there is no product, so every single interface frame is
  built.** The cap therefore inverts - it is set by compositing capacity, not
  by material depth, and the compositing method is the project's oldest
  unsolved problem and is still unproven on a real shot.

  **Decision: no more than eight composited interface shots in the film**, and
  the musician's lane is one of them and is shot first, because it is the
  hardest and because everything else is cheap to reschedule and it is not.
  Setting the number here rather than at storyboard is the whole reason
  RS-PRODUCT-05 sits at step 4 instead of step 7.

- **Setting and cast.** Three static desk-and-hands setups. Auckland interiors,
  real and unaltered, because the environment stays real and the interface is
  the only speculative layer. Three actors who are real creatives, varied by
  age, gender and background, consenting, none named in the report.

## D. Tone and craft

- **The dials.** Whimsy **2/10**. Edge **2/10**. Both deliberately low and both
  at the same value, which is unusual and is the point: the register is calm
  and confident rather than either playful or provocative. Seeded from "quiet
  and understated", "everything works beautifully", and the Apple-ad register.
  Honesty note carried from the step's own open items: the 1-to-10 dials have
  never been tested on a real project, so these numbers are a first use, not a
  calibrated reading.

- **North-star reference.** The Astra GPT-6 launch video, named by Polo. Second
  reference for one specific move only: Apple, "Great Ideas Start On Mac"
  (CV-0037) for holding on nothing until it earns the payoff.

- **Anti-reference.** Even Realities' own launch register - a machine reporting
  to you. Second anti-reference: Hyper-Reality, and the whole critical branch
  of the precedent set, because a film that reads as critique from frame one
  stops being a diegetic prototype.

- **Length.** 82 seconds (lock 4).

- **The word budget, and it sits outside every measured band.** SP-BUDGET-03
  gives 2.76 words per second for talking-delivery films and 2.2 to 2.4 for
  cinematic and motion-graphics-led ones. At 82 seconds those bands give 180 to
  226 words. **Neither applies here.** The record rules that actors speak
  "about their day rather than about the product, and not much of it", so
  speech is texture rather than spine, and there is no narration at all.

  **Decision: a ceiling of roughly 70 spoken words across the whole film**,
  about 0.85 words per second, which is a third of the lowest measured band.
  Stated as an out-of-band call with its reason, per PF-06's honesty habit.
  **This is a genuine gap in the brain**, not a local exception: there is no
  measured band for a sparse-dialogue film, and TNS has almost certainly made
  one. Logged in section H.

- **Segments.** SP-BUDGET-04 holds at any length: the hook takes under 10 per
  cent of the words, one "here is what this actually is" block takes about 25
  per cent, and segments run 8 to 10 seconds. At 82 seconds that is **8 to 10
  segments**, and the rule to obey when it gets tight is cut segments, never
  compress them.

- **Archetype.** `skit` **as the closest available slug, with the
  `openai-apple` register worn over it as a tone.**

  The reasoning, because this one is not clean. `skit` is the only slug that
  matches what is actually being made: a shoot with cast, nobody looking at
  camera, the product woven in and never explained. It fails on one word -
  `skit` means a short film with characters and a story, and this film has been
  ruled to have no story in that sense. The register Polo named belongs to
  `openai-apple`, which is the right feel and the wrong instructions, because
  `openai-apple` never shows a UI and this film is almost entirely UI.
  **The brain has no tone slug for the Apple-launch register** - `palantir` is
  the only tone it names. Logged in section H as a gap.

- **Feasibility, run in the order the step demands.**

  **PF-08 first, because it asks a different question.** Does this need intense
  3D modelling of hardware that does not exist? **No.** The glasses are a real
  physical prop and the interface is 2D compositing in After Effects. PF-08
  does not fire, and this is a clean pass rather than a close call.

  **PF-04, the two floors.** `skit` is the highest-cost entry of the ten in the
  ledger: actors, locations, film-score music, full sound design, and
  pre-production nobody else needs. The budget is NZD 1,000 to 1,500 for the
  entire semester. **By the ledger's own floors this film is not feasible.**

  It happens anyway because every input is donated: the director shoots it, the
  cast are friends, the location is a room, the music is a library licence.
  **PF-05 is the rule that bites, and it bites correctly** - founder-shot days
  are not free, the standard day rate gets imputed anyway, and the imputed
  number gets written down next to the decision. **Decision: write it down in
  the report.** For a capstone that is not a cost problem, it is a marks
  opportunity: a student who can say what the film would have cost at
  commercial rates has demonstrated exactly the professional framing the brief
  rewards.

  **PF-07, one video one shoot day.** Three characters, three setups, and
  PF-07 forbids a multi-day shoot for one video without an explicit exception
  call. **Decision: one day, one location dressed three times.** All three
  scenes are desk-and-hands by design, which is the reason the illustrator won
  the third slot in the first place. If that turns out to be impossible, the
  exception gets called out loud and written down, never assumed. **Needs
  Polo's ruling** (section G, item 5).

  **PF-06.** Every line above is an estimate against a ledger where nearly
  every number is tagged as one, and no PF- rule is outcome-validated. Said out
  loud, as the rule requires.

- **Ending and CTA.** Recorded as unresolved in the project. **Decision: the
  identity close.**

  FP-11 says the last five seconds get the same care as the first five, and
  FP-22 adds a third class beside act and ponder - the ending hands the viewer
  an identity to join, legal **only when the body has earned it**. The body
  earns it here about as directly as a film can, because the audience has just
  watched three people make three things.

  The line already exists and is the best sentence in the project:
  **"Compass does not create. You do."** It lands last, after the proof, which
  is FP-22's structure exactly. It also closes truth row A11, because the
  company name's resolution finally has a home a viewer actually meets.

  And it doubles as FP-11's way to act, in the room: the end card carries the
  Create Labs URL, the landing page is genuinely live, and the exhibition
  visitor can open it on their own phone while standing at the table. That is a
  real call to action for a fake product, which is a better exhibition object
  than a title card.

- **Must-include.** The empty lens, held. The guide lines going when the pencil
  stops. An empty centre in every single interface frame, with no exceptions,
  because one frame that breaks it breaks the argument. The identity close.

- **Must-avoid.** Any finished output on the lens. Compass speaking, or making
  any sound at all (truth row A13). Anything in the centre. A struggle beat, a
  failure beat or a moment of frustration. A price. An obvious critique in the
  first frame. A feature tour. Any AI-generated image anywhere in the chain
  that could reach a poster, since the poster ban is absolute and the hero
  frames come from this shoot. And the anti-painpoint guardrail from profile
  block 3: **never write a beat that wins the replacement argument.** The film
  may state its position; it may not defeat the opposing one.

  **Fixed across all four** (CP-SPREAD-03 wants this list written down before

- **The wildcard swing.** Adjacent-bold, not left-field. The reason is not

  the capstone on 31 July, and the fake product stack, which is being built as
  its own object rather than argued into the film. Nothing else is parked,
  because nothing else has been proposed that is too big.

## H. What was adapted, and why

The step assumes a paying client, a sold package and a spread of concepts going
out for selection. None of that exists. Five adaptations, named rather than
silently skipped, per the standing ruling that skipped steps get named.

1. **No founder check ran in parallel.** The framework's whole timing design is
   that step 3's check runs async while step 4 proceeds. Here the founder and
   the director are the same person, so nothing runs in parallel and both
   documents wait on the same conversation. The consequence is real: six truth
   rows are load-bearing for scenes, and step 6 cannot lock a concept resting on
   them.

2. **The count is 4 to 1, not 5 to 3.** CP-SPREAD-01 generates 4 to 5 and
   carries 3 to the client, because 3 is the proven client-facing count. There
   is no client, so carrying 3 is meaningless. But generating a set is not
   meaningless, for a specific reason: **the visual project report carries 40
   marks and is graded on process**, and three concepts considered and rejected
   with reasons are worth more in that report than one concept that appeared
   fully formed. So the generation half of the rule survives at full strength
   and the carry half collapses to one. The three cut concepts become report
   material, and the choice log at step 6 becomes a graded artefact rather than
   an internal record.

3. **The launch playbook's package rules were deliberately not loaded.**
   `packages.md` is about tier logic, the standard shoot kit, the funnel
   mapping and what a client buys. There is no package, no tier, no funnel and
   no buyer. Loading it would have produced ceremony. **One rule was taken
   across on its own merits:** LP-PKG-05's built-to-repurpose posture, which is
   why the 15-second loop is planned at concept time rather than discovered in
   the edit. `choreography.md` was not loaded either, for the same reason, and
   its placement-is-a-house-default ruling is the thing that does not transfer,
   since this project's placement is nothing like the house default.

4. **The client-direction playbook was not loaded.** It governs the concept
   call, pushback and persuading a client. There is no client to persuade. The
   nearest real equivalent - persuading the tutors and the stream coordinator
   about the prototype ruling - is a live and time-critical conversation, but it
   is an assessment negotiation and not a creative one, and running it through
   the client-direction rules would be a category error.

5. **The industry precedent guide was not loaded, and this is the one that
   deserves a note.** IP-CORE-01 governs how precedent is cited and graded,
   and the guide's cards are branched by industry slug - none of which fit.
   But the project has built its **own** precedent analysis, fifteen films
   deep, with provenance checked to a standard the brain's own guide would be
   pleased with. The honest position is that the project's precedent frame is
   better evidence for this film than the brain's cards are, and using the
   brain's grading discipline on the project's own set is the right move at
   step 5. That discipline in one line: cite the card and grade the claim out
   loud, and never let a pattern be introduced as proof.

## I. Gaps in the brain this run found

Recorded because the step's own design says a missing rule gets named rather
than invented around.

1. **There is no tone slug for the Apple-launch register.**
   `video-archetypes.md` names nine archetypes and exactly one tone,
   `palantir`. The `openai-apple` archetype carries the register this film
   needs, but as an archetype it forbids showing a UI, so it cannot be worn
   over `skit` the way `palantir` can. TNS has almost certainly made films in
   this register already. A second tone slug looks earned.

2. **SP-BUDGET-03 has no band for a sparse-dialogue film.** It measures
   talking-delivery at 2.76 words per second and cinematic or
   motion-graphics-led at 2.2 to 2.4. A film where speech is texture rather
   than spine sits at roughly a third of the lowest band, and nothing in the
   playbook covers it. The rule already says to re-measure as more films land
   and amend rather than defend, so this is an amendment waiting for a
   measurement.

3. **The viewer guide has no card for a consumer creator.**
   `consumer-prosumer` is on the watchlist and has never been earned, so a
   project whose named viewer is a beginner making things at home runs with no
   card at all. The watchlist entry already lists several corpus rows pointing
   the same way.

4. **RS-PRODUCT-05 assumes the product exists.** Its seconds cap is set by how
   deep the client's product material is. When there is no product and every
   frame is built, the cap has to be set by build capacity instead, which is a
   different quantity measured in a different unit. The rule handles thin and
   deep material; it has no pole for none.

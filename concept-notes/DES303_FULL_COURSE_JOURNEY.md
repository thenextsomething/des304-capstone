- Positionality must be woven naturally into blogs from Week 4 onward at key moments: setting goals, reflecting on outcomes, interpreting feedback, connecting to social issues.

What did excite him: video, storytelling, taking something raw and shaping it into something meaningful. **The Cebu trip.** A church exposure trip visiting sponsored children about a month before semester. Standing in homes that reminded him of his grandmother's, meeting a kid who dreamed of being a CEO while his sibling could not attend school due to an undiagnosable condition. The quote he carried back: "Sometimes I want to ask God why He allows poverty, famine, and injustice to happen when He could do something about it. But I'm afraid He might just ask me the same question." It did not change his career direction but added seriousness to why he wants to build things that matter.

**Interests mapping**: the through-line is building things and telling stories about them. Video, media, creative production, the startup ecosystem (founders, VC, Y Combinator). Most energised at the intersection of building companies, telling meaningful stories, and learning from people doing both at a high level.

**Demo directions considered**: (1) the art of storytelling for startups, or (2) showing his editing process at The Next Something.

Used mind mapping from his core interests. - Prepping the demo improved the actual internal processes at The Next Something (unexpected side effect).

## 3. Week 3: Demo Day (published 24 Mar)

Presented the real workflow with actual client files, live Miro boards, and storyboards. It landed: people were surprised to see inside real work.

**What went wrong**: covered four tools and moved too fast, so no depth on any single one. Explained broadly instead of going deep. The demo was about a fundamentally collaborative hands-on workflow, delivered in the least hands-on way possible.

**Peer feedback (4 reviewers, consistent)**: engaging, relevant, real-world examples strong, tool knowledge clear. Constructive thread: more time per tool, add a hands-on component.

**Watching others**:
- Rohan's Claude demo reframed Claude as more than a chatbot (connectors, designer use cases) and converted Polo from ChatGPT.
- Eddie's typography demo: deep knowledge, could have been more concise.
- Levi's Excel demo: the most participatory and fun; people were doing things from the start.
- Jay's Figma demo: hands-on, practical.

**The pattern**: demos that gave people something to do created the most energy and the most memorable takeaways. Going-forward rule for any presentation: start from "what do I want someone to be able to do by the end of this?"

## 4. The Jed Mentor Session (foundational, shaped everything after)

Key feedback from Jed on the initial idea (a promo video for speculative AR glasses for creatives):

1. **Clarify the core claim.** "Humans remain at the centre of creation even with AI assistance" is true to different extents in different contexts (passion projects vs mass marketing vs box-checking routine work vs distinctive product launches). The claim needs more specificity and nuance.
2. **Pin down the focus**: is it the product or the video? Polo confirmed the video is the deliverable. Jed's biggest practical contribution: separating **the artifact inside the concept** from **the actual medium of the assignment**.
3. **Avoid conceptual confusion**: keep the product being featured separate from the tools/process used to make the submission. If the AR glasses are both the subject and the means, the audience gets lost.
4. **The idea is solid and likely fits the brief**, but confirm with Leo and staff closer to the brief.
5. **Do not overcommit to the first concept.** Bench it and generate at least four more ideas.
6. **Be explicit about the emerging technology** the project itself engages with. AI video counts.
7. **Be careful with citation/documentation** if AI video is used.
8. It sits naturally in **speculative design** territory from a prior paper; talk to Levi and others with that experience, plus Yen.
9. **"Three concepts by end of S1"**: generate more than three, use experiments to narrow.
10. **Methodological scope** = choose a design methodology (double diamond, empathize-define-ideate-prototype-test, etc.).
11. **Make a project-specific HMW question** because the brief-level one is too broad. His two example directions: product-focused ("How might we use AR glasses to support the creative process?") or communication-focused ("How might we use video to communicate the complexities of emerging technology in a more compelling manner?").
12. Shared readings from Future Work and Play on speculative design.

Also from Jed, on blogging (Tutor_Feedback doc): write like a case study, separate yourself from your work ("you are not your work"), be blunt in evaluation, go deeper in analysis, reflective AND implementative.

And his brief-choosing advice that decided Week 4: find the brief that interests you most AND gives you freedom to pursue what you are already good at or passionate about. If nothing fully resonates, find the one with the most open space and make it yours.

## 5. Week 4: Picking a Lane (published 17 Apr)

Four streams on offer: Emerging Technologies, Business and Climate, Placemaking, Ageing and Community.

- Deliberately ignored the popularity of the community stream ("popularity is not a reason").
- The business stream, the obvious candidate given his life, did not resonate at all when actually read (environmental strategy, stakeholder engagement territory). Felt nothing, which told him everything.
- **Emerging Technologies won.** The words that jumped out: digital media, emerging technologies, creativity, and the sealing sentence "outcomes might include media artefacts." The brief's **diegetic prototype** concept (an artifact that looks and feels like it genuinely came from a future world) clicked immediately: high-quality intentional content creation with an academic and critical frame. Lens: **Create** (how emerging tech changes what it means to make things, be a creator, own your work).

**Wide ideation (unfiltered)**:
- A fake influencer who does not exist (fully AI-generated creator in 2040 Aotearoa, mockumentary or fake feed; commentary on authenticity and parasocial relationships)
- A retirement video from a human creator (2038, a NZ creator posts their final video because AI replaced them; emotional, quiet, simple to film)
- A tool where your AI collaborator co-owns your content and gets paid alongside you
- An app that certifies content was made by a human
- An AI that pitches creative ideas to you (flipping who is the creative in the room)
- A talent agency representing AI-generated artists

**Self-awareness note logged**: naturally drawn to ideas that already make sense in his startup/media world. Advantage (execution) and risk (avoiding genuine creative risk, dressing familiar work in new language). Wants actual speculation, not a polished version of what he already does.

## 6. Week 5: San Francisco, References, and the Wearable (published ~May)

**The San Francisco trip** (Mission/Dogpatch, visited the a16z office, met Lester Chen, head of creators; saw Waymos; every billboard about tech). Observations that shaped the whole project:
- Unbelievably young founders building things NZ has not heard of. Every startup wearing AI as a badge whether it fit or not.
- **The doomers**: founders and operators actively shipping AI products while genuinely believing AI might take over jobs, systems, everything. The tension between building and fearing what you build sits right inside the brief's question. Kept in the back pocket as a thread.
- SF sees things roughly five years early. The raw material for a speculative future Aotearoa is already visible there.

**References**: the brief's phrase "placing an audience inside a speculative scenario" describes a feeling, not a concept. Media that has done it to him: Black Mirror Bandersnatch, the Bear Grylls interactive special, choose-your-own-path games (Dispatch, Minecraft Story Mode). Goal: make the world feel like it exists. Not explained. Felt.

**Landed on**: video as the format, and the invented-product structure. Invent a product, build the world, make the launch film that lives inside that world. The product: a wearable in Apple Vision Pro space but built for the creative process. Ambient, present, notices when you are stuck. **It does not create, it only assists.** No name yet. Tone: quiet, understated. Cluely launch videos as a recurring reference.

## 7. Week 6: Starting to Actually Make Things (Crit 1)

**Crit 1 verdict, unanimous and humbling: start your experiments.** Stop planning, stop refining the concept, make something. No defence, they were right. Reflection: "there is a version of planning that is productive and a version that is just avoidance dressed up as being thorough."

Adopted the **double diamond** (Jed had named it) to structure a proper diverging week. Three experiments:

**Experiment: Learning Claude Code.** Tutorials, then built a simple assignment tracker (deliberately not capstone-related) to get a real read on the tool. Learnings: vagueness fails; think architecturally before opening the tool; front-load context; iterate in small steps; you are directing something, and if your vision is unclear the output reflects it.

**Experiment: AI image/video generation (Higgsfield, Gemini).** Natural-language descriptions produce outputs that are way off. **Discovery: use Claude to write the prompt first, then take that into the generator.** Massive quality difference. This became the prompt intermediary workflow used all semester and beyond. Also tried adding elements to existing images/footage: mixed results, useful data. Key insight: the tools reward people who already have a clear vision; otherwise they produce confident-looking noise. Thematically connected to the whole project.

**The theory breakthrough: the Emily is Away idea.** The tension worth putting someone inside is not "AI bad." It is the specific moment a creative stops trusting themselves and defaults to the machine. Emily is Away proved an interface itself can be the world. Concept: a game entirely inside a chat interface, player is a creative in future Aotearoa choosing at every turn between leaning on AI or trusting themselves. Pre-written responses (the AI in the game is a character, not a live system). Emerging tech in the making: Claude Code. The meta loop (AI tool building a game about AI reliance) is deliberate.

**Week 9 target set**: three strong presentable prototypes.

## 8. Week 7: Going Wide Again

Realised he was confusing familiarity with confidence on the two existing directions, so diverged once more (double diamond logic: if unsure whether you diverged enough, you did not).

**New format ideas**: fake social profile, fake news article, physical artifact, fake podcast/radio segment, fake job listing. The click: the journalism ones are not separate ideas, they are one media cycle. Article + radio + newspaper inside one website = one cohesive world. **The speculative journalism publication was born**: a fake outlet from ~2035 Aotearoa reporting from inside a world where the AI/creative tension has already settled. Familiar formats are powerful diegetic containers because the brain processes them as real before questioning them. Running The Next Something is why the format clicked: he knows intuitively what a credible publication looks and feels like.

**The piano experiment (the wearable's most honest expression).** Prototyped a pianist learning a piece with the wearable guiding in real time. Worked out the UI difference between ambient intelligence and "a piano app strapped to your face." Landed on: glowing keys just before you need them, floating note names, subtle timing pulse, edge progress bar. **"The device does not perform. It prompts."** The clearest articulation of the whole wearable concept.

**Two production approaches tried, both problematic**: full AI generation (no visual control) and self-filmed footage with AI-composited UI (the UI did not sit convincingly; compositing broke the believability it was meant to create). Concept clearer than ever, production method an open question.

## 9. Week 8: Three Ideas and the Reverse Brief (published 15 May; missed class, caught up independently)

**The three concepts as they stood**:
1. **Wearable AI Companion launch film**: shot headset experiments (painting, photography). Compositing AI UI overlays onto footage did not work; broke the illusion, which a fake launch film cannot afford. More AI-generated video attempts did not turn out well. Open questions: learn Premiere/After Effects, or rethink production entirely? A bad camera test planned to gauge how much production quality matters.
2. **Journalism publication**: basic version built in Claude Code (masthead, article, radio placeholder). Self-referential: emerging tech is both subject and tool.
3. **Emily is Away game**: pre-written branching, player chooses lean-on-AI vs trust-yourself, ending reflects the path, the game never says which was right.

**The reverse brief work** (Week 8 class tool; framing determines outcomes, name and test assumptions, defend your scope):
- **Focuses on**: putting an audience inside the tension between AI as creative collaborator and creative replacement, through speculative formats set in a future Aotearoa.
- **Not trying to**: take a side on AI, solve displacement, make a policy argument. The goal is making someone feel the weight of the choice.
- **Assumes**: everyday creators in Aotearoa already feel this tension unnamed, and speculative formats surface it better than documentary or argument.
- **Guiding value**: human authorship. The feeling that what you make is still yours.
- **Criticism anticipated**: his lens (startup optimist spaces, SF-shaped, international) vs the average NZ creative for whom the threat feels immediate; SF and Aotearoa are not the same context.
- **Defence**: the work does not represent his perspective on AI; it creates an experience and leaves the question open.

Realisation: none of the three concepts argue a position. They all create an experience.

**HMW question refined** (Jed had flagged the brief-level one as too broad): "How might we use speculative design to help everyday creators in Aotearoa feel what it means to work alongside AI, rather than be replaced by it?"

Theory anchors locked: Dunne & Raby (2013), Kirby (2010). APA 7th.

## 10. Week 9: The Crit and Developments (published 25 May; Crit 2)

**Went in with three ideas, came out with "two, honestly one and a half."**

- **The film landed hardest.** The room got it immediately. The reference that kept coming up: **the Apple Watch ad.** Cinematic, minimal, emotional. Feel what it is like to use the device, not understand how it works. Closest articulation yet of what he is making.
- **MUSE game received well but tempered**: V1 too short and mechanically limited to carry the weight of the concept. Became the clearest experiment brief.
- **Journalism publication died.** Could not answer: where does this go, what skill are you building, do you care long term? Directly asked how passionate he was, had nothing. Echoed Jed's advice about connecting to something you want a future with. Let it go.
- **Group push on the film**: design the physical product before choosing a production approach. Reframed the film from a compositing problem into a product design problem first.

**Crit takeaway**: pursue the directions where he has skin in the game. Films professionally = the launch film is a deepening, not a stretch. The game teaches an interactive mode of working he wants to understand.

**MUSE V2 rebuild** (fresh Claude Code instance, full structure mapped before any code):
- Four-file architecture (index.html, style.css, story.js, game.js); story.js separated so writing can be edited without touching code
- Three days: Day 1 concept/location scouting, Day 2 on set (planned shot failing, unplanned one might be better), Day 3 the edit (final call on the defining shot)
- Strictly one-on-one, no new characters
- Five endings on a 0-5 leanScore: The Purist (0-1), The Collaborator, The Negotiator, The Passenger, The Ghost (5). Each a fundamentally different relationship to authorship.
- Iteration rounds: auto-start opening ("I'm working on a short film and I'm kind of stuck. Can you help me develop the concept?" because that is how someone actually opens ChatGPT); fixed choice buttons with keyboard support; retimed everything (2000ms typing indicator, 800ms message gap, 1200ms pre-choice pause, 2500ms auto-next)
- **UI corruption**: CSS custom properties shift with leanScore. Clean/warm/minimal at low score; cold blue-black, purple-tinted MUSE bubbles, scanlines, vignette, filling influence bar at high score. The interface reflects the transfer of creative autonomy. Felt, not told.

**Film-side work**: installed After Effects, orientation via tutorials. Rough scene ideas, lighting thoughts, device-on-camera thinking. **Wearable landscape research**: Meta Ray-Ban (disappearing consumer wearable), Brilliant Labs Frame (minimal, design-forward, closest to the target), Xreal (immersive end), Snap Spectacles (creative-first positioning). Direction: closer to Frame than Vision Pro; lighter, signals collaboration not dominance.

**Theory**: Apple ads sell emotional truth and identity, not features. Dunne & Raby: speculative design opens a space for the audience to ask whether they want the future, and the launch film format does exactly that.

10. **The Aotearoa Creative journalism prototype** (built in Claude Code, then deliberately killed at Crit 2).

## 13. Final Presentation (Week 12) and the Feedback

**Peer feedback received and Polo's own IRC reflection on it**:

- **What was shakier**: people wanted more of "how he got to the idea." He showed outcome more than journey; the experiments existed but were not sequenced clearly enough on the slides. Pacing was uneven, which made the process story harder to follow.

Fewer dense slides, deliberate beats, rehearse out loud against a timer. Under-showing the messy middle undersells the learning.

## 14. The Big Threads (What Actually Changed Over 12 Weeks)

1. **From one idea to three and back to one (plus a half).** Week 4-5: the wearable film. Week 6-7: diverged into the game and the publication. Week 9: the publication killed by the passion test, the film confirmed as primary, the game as secondary. Jed's "bench the first idea and generate more" advice was vindicated: the diverging did not replace the original direction, it stress-tested and strengthened it.
2. **From explaining to experiencing.** Every surviving concept puts an audience inside the tension rather than arguing a position. The through-line from Bandersnatch to Emily is Away to the Apple Watch ad.
3. **From planning to making.** Crit 1's unanimous "start your experiments" broke the avoidance-dressed-as-thoroughness pattern. Everything valuable afterward came from making.
4. **The process discoveries compound.** Prompt intermediary workflow, architectural thinking in Claude Code, story/code separation, product-design-before-production. All reusable in DES304 and at The Next Something.
5. **Failure was productive on schedule.** The compositing failures, the Suno dead end, V1's shallowness, the publication's death: each one either redirected the work or defined the next experiment. The course's framing (experiments test assumptions, failed experiments are valid learning) played out literally.
6. **Positionality became a working tool, not a disclaimer.** SF optimist lens vs Aotearoa reality, named in the reverse brief, tested in crits, flagged again in final feedback. The standing move: name it, bridge it.

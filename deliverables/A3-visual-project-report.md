## What it is

A visual project report that clearly communicates the entire design journey, combining text, diagrams, graphics and photographs.

The brief states directly that **there is no evaluation of the Miro board**. The board is a working space. You are expected to curate and organise the process material from Miro and other sources into one cohesive, accessible, printed report.

## Format and structure

- A4 PDF, portrait or landscape, your choice
- Around 50 pages, described as flexible if needed
- No strict word count, but the content must be clear, concise and focused
- Designed however you prefer, but it must be well organised, visually engaging, and aligned with the design methodology you chose. The brief gives Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver as its example, which is the double diamond
- Structure the content with clear titles and subtitles within those stages
- This is a **visual** report, so prioritise diagrams, graphs, sketches and images to summarise and support ideas

## Deliverables

1. Submit a digital A4 PDF, optimised, around 50 pages
2. **Print and bind a physical book** to exhibit alongside the prototype
3. No strict word count, keep it clear, concise and focused

## The five core questions it must answer

### Why?
- Why does this problem matter, and why should we care
- Background and context of the chosen challenge
- Brief summary of the original and refined How Might We questions
- Key research findings and insights that informed the design direction

### Who?
- Who are you, and why did you choose this project
- How does it relate to your personal values and positionality
- Who are the stakeholders impacted by or benefiting from the project
- Who is the intended audience or user group, supported with persona tools or relevant demographic and secondary data

### What?
- What is the proposed solution, and what does it look like
- Show the process through early sketches, photos of prototypes, diagrams, branding mock-ups, final renders or models

### How?
- How does the proposal function
- Are there steps, user interactions, or system flows
- Include relevant design documentation such as system maps, storyboards, service blueprints, or technical diagrams

### Impact
- What changes does the project propose
- What are the key social, cultural, environmental or economic impacts
- Use an impact framework taught in class, with Theory of Change given as the example

## Additional required sections

- **Cover page:** project title, subtitle, your name, stream, year, Design Programme, small UoA logo
- **Project summary (abstract):** 120 to 300 words, the problem and the value the solution brings
- **Contents page or index**
- **Reference to brief:** summary of the original brief, the HMW question, and the refined HMW question
- **Methodology and timeline:** description of the design methodology and timeline followed
- **Research findings:** summarised with visual elements such as diagrams, graphs and key quotes
- **Cultural considerations and positionality:** reflect on positionality, values, and engagement with Te Tiriti o Waitangi
- **Process documentation:** early sketches, prototypes and so on

The cultural positionality criterion names five principles explicitly:

- **Whaanau:** relationships and collective belonging
- **Kaitiakitanga:** protection and care
- **Mahi tahi:** collaboration and partnership
- **Hanga:** creation through purposeful action
- **Huri hanga:** diversity, plurality and transformation

## What this means for Compass

This is my reading of the brief against where the project currently is, not part of the brief itself.

**The report is worth four times the film's presentation.** At 40 marks it is the largest single thing this semester, and it is a design object in its own right, not a write-up. It needs the same visual standard as the film.

**Everything on the Miro board has to be redrawn for print.** The board is explicitly not marked. The decision log, the feedback log, the twelve weekly frames and the timeline all become source material to be curated. Filing material into the weekly frames each Friday is what makes this possible later, so the existing working habit is doing real work toward 40 marks.

**The double diamond is now load-bearing.** The methodology was approved on 4 August, and the report's biggest criterion at 10 marks asks for the stages of the chosen methodology to be clearly demonstrated with the design inquiry tools that produced insights at each stage. Discover, Define, Develop and Deliver need actual documented activity underneath each one.

**Two sections have no material yet.** The impact framework, with Theory of Change named as the example, and the Te Tiriti positionality reflection against those five principles. Together they carry 10 marks. The Impact planning workshop on 22 September should supply the first. The positionality workshop from 4 August feeds the second. Neither exists in the repo yet.

**Positionality already has strong raw material.** The standing positionality notes cover The Next Something, the San Francisco influence, and the gap between a startup-optimist view of AI and the average New Zealand creative's view. The report needs that woven in, and the project's existing rule is that positionality is woven through rather than parked in its own section. Note the tension with the brief, which lists cultural considerations and positionality as a named required section. Safest reading: have the named section, and let the reflection also run through the document.

**The prototyping process criterion rewards the failures.** Five marks for showing how ideas evolved in response to feedback, with photos of tests, early diagrams and experiments. The compositing attempts that did not work, the OpenCV script that dropped UI elements, the AI generation that produced uncontrollable output, all of that is worth marks rather than being something to hide.

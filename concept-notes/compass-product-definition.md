# Compass: the product definition

The single source of truth for what Compass is. Everything else gets built from this: the script, the interface design, the landing page, the press kit and the posters. Written 12 September 2026 from Polo's answers on the Capstone HQ page.

**Compass** is a pair of AI glasses built for the moment you are making something.
Made by **Create Labs**, Auckland, New Zealand. Launching 2028.

---

## The one rule

**Compass does not create. It only assists.**

Not as a policy the company chose, and not as a safety setting that could be switched off. It is what the thing is. A human has to be making something for there to be anything to assist.

In Polo's words:

> "It's like using an agent without the human. It literally just won't work. You need a human to prompt the agent, and this is going to be the same thing."

## The three states

This is the product's whole argument, and it is also the film's structure.

| State | What the wearer sees |
|---|---|
| **Off** | Ordinary glasses. |
| **On, not working** | **Nothing.** Put Compass on and the lens is empty. No home screen, no notifications, no widgets, no clock. |
| **Working** | It wakes, because you started. Quiet, specific help for the thing your hands are already doing. |

The empty middle state is the feature, not the absence of one. Every other product in this category fills your vision the moment you put it on. Compass waits for you.

## What it does, by discipline

**Shared across all three**

- A reference layer: moodboards, previous work, and work a client liked, called up beside what you are making.
- A customisable layer. You choose what appears, because every creative works differently. No two people's lenses look the same.
- Nothing in the centre of your vision. Ever.

**For the photographer and videographer**

- A shot checklist on the lens, showing what you have taken and what you still owe.
- A review of the last shot without leaving the camera.
- Reference frames and moodboards from the brief.
- A light reading, with a small directional diagram.

**For the musician**

- The next notes lighting up on the keys, a second or two ahead of where your hands are.
- A timing pulse you feel rather than watch.
- A quiet progress line at the edge of the session.

**For the illustrator**

- Proportion and perspective guides that appear only while the pencil is actually moving, and fade the moment it stops.
- A reference card sliding in from a relevant artist, named and dated.
- Visual weight nudges, as soft text that fades in and out.

## What Compass will never do

The list that protects the rule. Nothing on it appears on screen at any point in the film.

- It never produces finished work. No generated image, no written line, no completed bar of music.
- It never acts before you do. There is no suggestion waiting for you when you put it on.
- It never fills the centre of your vision.
- It never speaks. Compass has no voice, in the film or in the product.
- It never shows you someone else's finished version of what you are attempting.

## What it sees, and who owns that

**Everything is processed on the device, and nothing is kept by default.** The only things retained are what you deliberately save into your own reference library. Nothing leaves the glasses unless you send it. Create Labs never sees how you work.

This is the product's second real differentiator after the assist-only rule, and it is the design decision that carries the Te Tiriti section of the report: kaitiakitanga is protection and care, and the record of how a person makes things is the thing being protected. It also happens to be exactly what a New Zealand company would build and what a San Francisco one probably would not.

## Specification, for the fact sheet

Plausible for 2028, and deliberately unremarkable, because the product is meant to read as eyewear rather than hardware.

| | |
|---|---|
| Weight | 38 grams |
| Frame | Titanium, matte black |
| Lens | Prescription-ready, light reactive |
| Display | Binocular waveguide, monochrome, full colour on the reference layer |
| Battery | A full working day |
| Processing | Entirely on device |
| Storage | Your reference library only |
| Availability | New Zealand first, 2028. Other markets to follow. |
| Price | Announced at launch. |

## The company

**Create Labs.** Founded in Auckland by a group of working creatives who teamed up with engineers, because both sides wanted to see what AI could do for making things rather than instead of making things.

The name is about the customer, not the product. **Compass does not create. You do.** That line belongs on the landing page and it resolves the name before anyone has to wonder about it.

New Zealand is the deliberate first market. Create Labs is launching at home before going to the United States, which is the normal and sensible play for a New Zealand company going overseas, and it is the reason this is a New Zealand launch film rather than a global one filmed here.

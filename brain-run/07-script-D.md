## 0. Read this before the script

Three things, so nothing below is taken as settled.

**This step is gated and the gate is red.** The truth sheet's own gate table says
step 7 is "hard-blocked on the fiction rows" and rows A1, A3, A4, A5 and A6 are all
PENDING. Concept D leans on all five. I have written anyway, on the same explicit
authorisation that let step 5 run against a DRAFT brief, and under the same
condition: **I have invented no new answers.** Every blocked beat is written on the
recommendation the truth sheet already carries for that row, and every one of them
is flagged inline as `[UNCONFIRMED: A<n>]` so a ruling shows immediately which shot
it changes. Nothing here survives a ruling that goes the other way without a
rewrite, and section 8 says exactly which shot dies with which row.

**The film fits the compositing cap, and the concept card said it would not.** The
card estimated eight to ten composited interface shots. This script lands on
**six builds across seven shots**, and section 6 shows the working. The reason is
not cleverness, it is staging: two of the three refusals are proved by practical
props that cost nothing to composite, and the illustrator's whole refusal and
payoff run as one continuous take with the state change inside it. The honest
qualifier is in section 6 too, and it matters: by build hours rather than by shot
count this film still sits at the ceiling, because that one continuous illustrator
take is the hardest thing in the film by a distance.

**The thing this script was most careful with.** Truth row A4 says the word
"corrections" on the illustrator's lens reads as a machine marking her work wrong,
and that she is the character chosen precisely because illustrators are the most
recognisable face of the AI replacement fear. Nothing in refusal three evaluates
her drawing. Nothing circles, highlights, measures, scores, flags, overlays or
compares her marks. The signal blue never touches a line she has made. What
Compass does in her scene is hold structure in front of the page and then clear
out of the way of her hand, and the reference photograph sits in its own panel and
stays there. Section 4's screen directions say this as a hard negative under the
shot, so an editor or a compositor cannot drift into it by accident.

## 3. The five standing rules this script obeys in every single shot

Written here once so they do not have to be repeated fifteen times.

1. **The centre of every lens frame is empty.** No exceptions. One frame that
   breaks it breaks the film's argument.
2. **Nothing Compass draws ever sits on top of finished human work.** Not the
   drawing, not the photograph, not a played note.
3. **The interface exists only in point of view.** Third person never shows lens
   contents. This is a shooting rule and a compositing saving at the same time.
4. **The type rule.** The film's own type and the lens language have to be
   impossible to confuse, because two of the film's type cards land over
   third-person frames. **Lens type:** translucent glass panel, soft blur behind
   it, thin light edge, held in the wearer's eyeline. **Film type:** no panel, no
   blur, no edge, set flat on the frame in the safe margin, one weight, warm white
   #FAF4EA. If a viewer cannot tell the two apart at a glance the design has
   failed and the film's whole argument goes with it.
5. **The colour rule.** Warm white #FAF4EA is the base. **Signal blue #6E8FC7 is
   only ever on the one thing Compass is actively offering in that moment** - the
   key face at the moment to press it, the guide while the pencil is moving, the
   frame that just appeared. Everything else on the lens is warm white. The
   reference layer is the single full colour exception. `[the blue's value is
   proposed, not ruled - product definition, 16 September]`

## 6. The compositing count, worked honestly

The cap is eight, set at step 4, still unruled (item 6). Here is every build.

| Build | Shot | What is composited | Why it costs what it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1** | 2 | the musician's lane arriving: columns off the keys, descending blocks with note names, piece-and-progress panel, metronome | the hardest interface in the film to sit convincingly on a real object, which is why the brief says shoot it first |
| **C2** | 4 | the key face lighting, waiting, and going out | macro, one small element, short. The cheapest shot in the film and the most important |
| **C3** | 5 | the lane running, four keys lighting and going under her fingers | a rebuild of C1 at a different focal length, with hand occlusion |
| **C4** | 8 | the photographer's four panels, plus the last photo arriving with its blue edge | the widest the lens ever gets. Four panels plus an arrival, one plate |
| **C5** | 9 | the checklist, close, one tick landing | a new angle on the same panel, so a new build, but a small one |
| **C6** | 11 and 13 | the illustrator: guide lines arriving on pencil-down, the reference panel with matching divisions, the recommendations stack, the fade-ahead as her hand moves, then the whole lens going on pencil-up | **one build covering 21 seconds of screen time.** One continuous take, one camera position, shot 12 cut into the middle of it |

**Six builds, seven shots, against a cap of eight. It fits, with room.**

**Now the qualifier, because the count alone is misleading.** C6 is 21 seconds of
continuous point-of-view compositing with a moving head, a moving hand occluding
the graphics, and an element that has to react to where her pencil is going. By
build hours it is worth roughly three ordinary shots on its own, which puts the
real load at about eight. **So this film sits AT the ceiling by effort even though
it sits under it by count, and it sits there on the one shot that cannot be
rescheduled, because the illustrator's refusal and the film's payoff are both
inside it.** The concept card's estimate of eight to ten was right about the work
and wrong about the number.

**The fallback if C6 will not come off, and it should be decided before the shoot
rather than during it.** Split C6 into two locked-off point-of-view shots: one for
the guide lines arriving and the fade-ahead, one for the vanish, with the head
position held still in both so the track is simple. That is seven builds instead of
six, each of them far easier, and the cost is that the payoff arrives on a cut
instead of inside the same breath as the refusal, which is a real loss of power at
the film's best moment.

**What I did NOT do to get the count down.** I did not cut a refusal. The concept
card warned that cutting one takes the structure from three legs to two, and it is
right. Nothing here is thinner than the card described.

**What is NOT counted in the cap, said out loud so nobody discovers it later.** The
two type cards (shots 3, 14) and the end card (shot 15) are film graphics, not
interface composited into a plate, so they do not spend the cap. They do spend
design time. And the empty lens in shots 1 and 14 costs nothing at all, which is
the quiet joke at the centre of this whole project.

## 7. The two format extensions, named

SP-STYLE-05 fixes the house format and this script keeps all of it: the legend, one
section per segment of the loaded structure, a one-line why-line under each header
written for the reader rather than for us, italics for what is on screen, a runtime
marker. Two additions:

1. **Numbered shots with durations and a running total**, because the client here is
   the director and the shoot, and a 56-word film cannot be paced off its dialogue.
   The house format assumes words carry the timing. Here images do.
2. **A hard negative under two shots** (9 and 11/13). These are the two beats where
   a compositor doing the obvious thing would break the film's argument, and the
   note belongs where the shot is, not in a covering email. This is SP-STYLE-07's
   discipline applied in reverse: a choreography note says what enters and from
   where, and these two say what must never enter at all.

## 8. What this script breaks, leans on, and costs

### 8.1 The gate it ran through

Step 7 is hard-blocked on the PENDING fiction rows and this run went ahead anyway,

### 8.2 Brain rules this script breaks, knowingly

- **SP-LAUNCH-01.** PROBLEM is deleted, not collapsed. The rule's only sanctioned
  collapse is when the pain IS the hook, which merges HOOK and PROBLEM rather than
  removing PROBLEM. Here the pain is gone, because the register was ruled to have
  no struggle and no failure beat. This is a property of the project's rulings and
  not of this concept.
- **FP-21 and FP-14.** The villain never appears on screen and the viewer is never
  made to feel the imbalance directly. What this concept does instead is dose the
  negative once per scene as the thing that almost happened: the lit key nobody
  presses, the list nobody writes, the guide line that clears out of the way. That
  is FP-21's salt-not-flour instruction followed at the level of design rather than
  drama, and it is the strongest answer available under the no-struggle ruling, but
  it is not what either rule asks for.
- **The 12 September intercut ruling.** The record says the three wearers are
  intercut, not run one after another. **This script runs them in order**, because
  the refusals escalate and an escalation that is shuffled stops escalating. What
  the film does instead is rhyme: each scene opens on a person already wearing and
  already working, and the last live-action frame answers the first. **If Polo holds
  the intercut ruling**, the cheapest fix is to plant a two-second third-person
  frame of each of the other two inside the musician's scene, paid for out of the
  holds in shots 4 and 5, which costs nothing to composite and costs four seconds of
  the film's best silence.
- **The putting-on ritual.** Nobody puts the glasses on anywhere in this film. The
  record says the opening scenes exist "to introduce the characters and to show them
  putting the glasses on". The brief's own written fixed list says only "third-person
  openings", which this script keeps. That contradiction is unresolved in the
  documents themselves and concept D needs it resolved in the brief's favour.
- **The statistics passage.** World-building plans "a small part of the film goes
  over the numbers". **There is no statistics passage in this script, and that is
  deliberate.** The truth sheet's section 4 has the 65 per cent sitting on the wrong
  base, the AI Forum numbers being used about creatives when they are about all
  industries, and seven citations that cannot currently be stood up. A film has even
  less room for a footnote than a poster does, and the honesty line says every number
  appears at the strength its source supports with its real base named. Dropping the
  numbers is the sheet's own logic applied.
- **SP-BUDGET-03.** 0.41 spoken words per second against a lowest measured band of
  2.2. Out of band, said out loud, already logged by the brief as a gap in the brain
  rather than a local exception.
- **PF-04.** `skit` is the most expensive of the ten entries in the cost ledger and
  the semester budget is NZD 1,000 to 1,500. By the ledger's own floors this film is
  not feasible. PF-05 is why it proceeds and 8.5 is where the imputed number gets
  written down.
- **SP-STYLE-06, and this one needs naming properly.** The rule says no proof beat
  without a source moment: every feature beat is a tightened replay of something a
  client actually showed us on a call. **There is no client and there were no calls.**
  Every moment in this script is built from the pinned product definition and the
  project record instead. That is the honest substitute, not a pass, and it is the
  same adaptation step 3 made when it swapped its two questions.

**On segment count.** SP-BUDGET-04 wants 8 to 10 segments of 8 to 10 seconds each at
this runtime. This script has 7 named segments and three of them run 11, 16 and 17
seconds. The break is real. The defence is that a refusal is one beat and needs its
setup, its almost and its release, which cannot be done in ten seconds, and that the
rule's own instruction when it gets tight is to cut segments rather than compress
them, which is what deleting PROBLEM did. Counted a second way, by shots grouped into
setup and refusal, the film reads as nine units of 5 to 11 seconds, which is inside
the band. Both counts are here rather than the flattering one.

### 8.4 Its honest weakness

**A near-miss is a thought, and thoughts are slower than images.** This is the same
weakness the concept card named and writing the thing has not removed it, it has only
located it precisely. The film asks a viewer to notice three times that something did
not happen. A viewer who does not notice sees three features working normally and goes
home with a perfectly nice film about AI glasses and no argument at all.

Having written it, I can say exactly where the risk concentrates, which the concept
stage could not:

- **Refusal one is safe.** A lit key with nothing on it, held, then a finger. Six
  seconds, one element, no reading required. If the film works, this shot is why.
- **Refusal three is safe for a different reason.** The fade-ahead is a continuous
  behaviour rather than a single event, so a viewer who misses it once has three more
  chances inside the same take. And the payoff is not a near-miss at all, it is a
  disappearance, which is the most legible thing in the film.
- **Refusal two is the weak leg, and it is weak in the format that matters most.** It
  needs the viewer to read a paper brief at 0:30 and hold those words in their head
  until 0:39. On the landing page and in the presentation that is fine. **On a silent
  exhibition loop, watched by a visitor who arrived halfway through, it is close to
  invisible.** The refusal that most needs the viewer to read is sitting in the film
  built for a viewer who will not.

Two smaller ones, honestly:

- **The one rule is proved hardest once.** Only the musician gets a full empty-to-awake
  wake. The photographer and the illustrator get smaller versions, the blue-edged frame
  arriving because he pressed the shutter and the guides arriving because her pencil
  touched down. That is by design, because this concept's spine is the boundary and not
  the wake, but it means the film's headline claim is carried by twelve seconds at the
  front.
- **Three rooms in one room.** Everything in section 2 is achievable and none of it is
  comfortable. If the three scenes read as the same room, the film stops being three
  lives and becomes one set, and the intercut ruling it already broke was the thing
  that would have hidden it.

**Imputed at TNS's own ledger rates** (the cost-input table, currency as the ledger

21-second continuous point-of-view composite, which is closer to the `general-mg` entry's
named overrun pattern than to a standard edit: each feature shown is singular and paced,
so features added at scripting are hours added. A realistic commercial quote for this
film is above the ledger's arithmetic, and the honest way to say that in the report is to
say the ledger has no receipt for it rather than to invent a multiplier.

For a capstone that is not a cost problem, it is a marks opportunity, and it is the
reason PF-05 exists.

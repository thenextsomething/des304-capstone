# Storyboard review with the director: the film gets a problem phase, and the rooms get settled

**Date:** Friday 2 October 2026, afternoon
**Who:** Polo and Josiah, who is directing and shooting the film.
**Source:** Polo's Granola recording, titled "Compass". Every voice in that transcript carries the same label, so lines are attributed by context rather than by a speaker name, and anything quoted below is verbatim. Other people were in and out of the room and the recording runs on past the film into unrelated work, which is not part of this project and is not covered here.

**This note records what was said and decided two days before the shoot. It deliberately does not change the product definition or the script, because four of the things decided in this meeting sit against rules already written down and Polo has not ruled on those yet. They are listed at the bottom as open.**

---

## The headline: the film now has a problem phase

Each character struggles at their craft for roughly the first twenty seconds. Then they put the glasses on. Only after that does the first point of view shot happen and the first interface appear.

Josiah's reasoning, in his own words:

> "there needs to be like the problem, which is like, I need guidance as opposed to someone telling me what to do. I just need guidance."

And on where the first point of view shot lands, which is the part that makes it a structural change rather than a dressing change:

> "They're going through the problem, they're about to do it, and then they're like, okay, fuck this, let me just put this thing on."

Then the first interface, then the photographer's, then the painter's.

A note for the record rather than a decision: the script already holds its first eighteen seconds for three people putting the glasses on and nothing happening. So what the problem phase changes is what fills that stretch, not how long it is. What it does change is the reason the audience is watching it, which goes from a thing the film is withholding to a thing each character needs.

## The three problems

- **Pianist.** Looking at the sheet music. Josiah's version of the beat: "Her maybe looking at the, the sheet being like, what the fuck, what even is this?"
- **Painter.** How to blend colours, and how to get certain colour tones. This one came out of a real person rather than out of the room, which is why it is the strongest of the three: *"That's all I hear from Chloe is like how to get certain color tones."* Chloe is the painter.
- **Photographer.** How to use the camera. "it's all like, what f-stop do I have to be at? ... What ISO?"

## The opener, and the reasoning behind it

> "if you want a good opener for any sort of film, you want to show the environment."

The shots, in order: a plant in the foreground with the pianist blurred behind at the piano, cut to her hands on the piano, then the lead into the first note, then cut to her eyes, and his reason for that last cut is the useful bit, which is "to then connect that to her".

Then the three characters are introduced in sequence: pianist, photographer, painter. Act one stays third person throughout, confirmed in the room: "Only POV when we go to the glasses."

## Why glasses rather than an app

This came up unprompted and it is the clearest answer the project has to the obvious objection, so it goes down verbatim:

> "that kind of exists already. There's an app that you can get on... iPad. But the thing about this is like, we want to show you it's integrated. A pair of glasses can do everything."

## The one rule, restated by the director without being told it

Talking about the piano interface, Josiah arrived at the assist-only rule on his own:

> "instead of having to read these notes, you're just... you're still the main thing. Cause the glasses would be nothing without [you]."

That is worth more than the project restating its own rule, because it is someone outside the project reading the interface and landing on the point it was built to make.

## Locations, now settled

- **Pianist:** indoors, upstairs, natural light.
- **Photographer:** starts indoors, with the camera sitting forgotten on a shelf or in a drawer, then moves outside to the wharf. The reason for the indoor beat is characterisation rather than scenery: "we need to establish he's a new photographer... he hasn't touched it and he's decided to pick it up today". The garage is dropped.
- **Painter:** indoors, the living room, chosen for natural light and "some pretty angles".

## The photographer is a hobbyist

Real estate photographer was raised and dropped. What he is instead is someone who photographs "the beauty of life, the way that the sun hits the wall". The beat that carries it is him looking around the room working out what is worth taking, which Josiah put as every photographer being able to relate to not knowing what to point the camera at.

## The painting, and the continuity problem solved

Two canvases. Chloe paints one to a midway state and one finished, so the film has a genuine before and after and nobody has to come back for a second shoot. Each takes her about an hour. Both she and Polo's sister already have paint supplies, so nothing has to be bought for it.

There is deliberately no physical reference for the painting in the room, and the reason is the product: "I don't think there should be a reference because the reference is on here", meaning on the glasses. That lines up exactly with what the record already says the painter's reference is, which makes it the one decision in this meeting that strengthens an existing rule rather than pulling against it.

## Shooting plan

- One character each, and the setup is the main work.
- After the setup it is "B-roll, B-roll, B-roll, just get whatever looks really nice".
- Lots of point of view for all three.
- One hard rule, stated flatly: **"reveal POV needs to have their hands."**

## The interface is left open on purpose

> "we can actually be creative with what appears on the UI, but we can always do that after."

Two ideas raised and not settled: a reference shot, and a connection to the camera so the photographer can see his last shot. Note that the second of those is already a confirmed Compass feature from Navia's conversation back in July, so it is a rediscovery rather than a new idea.

## Kit, and the day

- An Amaran 200X, plus Josiah's own light, a 200D or similar.
- **Filming day: Sunday 4 October.** The plan of record had the shoot on Friday 2 October, so it has moved by two days.
- A possible prop run to Glenfield Mall beforehand, walking around rather than working off a list. Paint supplies are not on it, because both painters already have theirs.

## Four things this meeting puts against the written record

These are open, not settled. Each one is a real conflict with something already decided, and the shoot is on Sunday, so they want a ruling rather than a discussion.

**1. The problem phase sits against the no-struggle rule.** The register was ruled on 12 September as an Apple-style product launch where everything works beautifully, with the words "No struggle, no failure beat. It is a product launch, not a drama" (`world-building.md`), and the concept work carries it as a rule that never bends. Three characters visibly struggling at their craft is a change to that. The likely resolution, offered as something to consider rather than as a finding: the rule survives if it is restated as *Compass* never struggling, with the person's own difficulty being the thing that creates the need, which is how product advertising normally works. That keeps the rule's actual purpose, which was never to say nobody in the film has a problem, it was to stop the film winning the replacement argument for the other side.

**2. The voiceover sits against the silent-loop rule.** The film is specified to work with the sound off, because it plays on a loop at a public exhibition where nobody is wearing headphones, and the record pushes that further by saying there is no narration anywhere. This meeting adds a voiceover over the three interface reveals, and the reason given for it is attention: "Which will keep the attention." A voiceover is fine if it is doing a second job on top of a film that already reads silently. It is not fine if it becomes the thing carrying the story, because the exhibition version would then be carrying nothing. The test is whether the cut still works muted.

**3. "Piano tiles IRL."** Josiah's own phrase for the piano interface, arrived at in the room. The project already has this recorded as its first eye-roll risk, that notes falling toward keys reads as the Guitar Hero mechanic and somebody will say so out loud, and the answer on the record is that what keeps it assistance is that she presses every key herself and none of it starts until she is already playing. Naming an actual game sharpens the risk rather than creating it. Worth deciding before Sunday whether anything in the framing or the timing has to work harder to keep it on the assistance side.

**4. "The UI can be anything."** Said twice in the meeting, and it is not actually true of this project. The lens has eleven allowed elements, an empty centre, five refusals on the never-do list, and five decisions ruled on 27 September specifically so the shoot would build off one version of the truth. What the director does have freedom over is framing, and which of those elements appear in a given shot. What he does not have freedom over is what Compass is allowed to do. That distinction has not been said to him in those words yet.

## The one question the recording raises, and what happened to it

**Who is playing the pianist.** The recording does not say it outright. One line reads as though Polo is playing the pianist himself, and another reads as though Josiah is playing the photographer, which is the only way both lines work together. Nothing in the transcript settles it, so it is not something this note is going to decide off a reading.

It got ruled the same day rather than left hanging. Polo on the musician, Josiah on the photographer, Chloe on the painter, written up in `world-building.md` and stamped on the script. So the question is recorded here as where it came from, not as something still open.

Two things that follow from it are worth knowing, and both are logged over there rather than here. Two of the three wearers are men who work together making films, which is close to the thing the casting variety note exists to prevent, so that note now carries a dated question for Polo. And Chloe is a source as well as a performer, because the painter's colour problem in the film is a real difficulty of hers, which is why it is the strongest of the three problems.

## Actions arising

- Rule on the four conflicts above, or at least on the first two, before Sunday.
- Answer the casting variety question now logged in `world-building.md`, because the fix for a no is a swap and a swap is cheap now and impossible after the shoot.
- Say the interface boundary to Josiah in plain words: free on framing and on which elements appear in a shot, not free on what Compass does.
- Once the first two are ruled, the product definition and the script get updated. Nothing in them was changed by this meeting.

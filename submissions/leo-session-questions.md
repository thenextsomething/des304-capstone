# Leo's session, Tuesday 15 September

The schedule calls it "Final Presentations and Showcase info session, A3 submission expectations, submission plans draft". It is the designated room for these, and there is no equivalent session after it.

You will not get through all of them. The first four are the ones that change what you do next.

---

## Ask these four

**1. Does the fake product stack satisfy the prototype requirement?**

Frame it as a statement, not a question. "Here is what I am making, does this meet the prototype requirement." You get a ruling on your actual plan rather than a general answer.

What you are making: a live landing page and website for Create Labs, a printed press kit, the posters, and the social presence as designed mockups. The glasses on camera are a plain pair of frames.

The bit to name yourself before he does: the brief says a physical prototype is expected even for an intangible outcome, and half the marks are fabrication, tools, materials and craftsmanship. A website is not fabrication. Ask him directly whether that is a problem.

Your fallback, offer it: dress the bought frames properly with tinted lenses and an etched wordmark and present them as a finished object alongside the stack.

*Worth 15 marks and it decides the next five weeks of work.*

**2. Can a still frame from the film be a poster hero shot?**

The posters ban AI-generated images. Your hero shots are real footage with an interface composited on top in After Effects, some of it built with AI motion graphics tools.

So: is a composited frame an AI-generated image, a photograph with graphics on it, or something in between? And does an AI-assisted upscale or clean-up cross the line?

*This decides your entire build order to 15 October. If the answer is no, every hero shot has to be straight photography and the shoot changes.*

**3. The showcase, since you will not be there.**

You have emailed him, so this is the in-person follow-up. Is attendance required or marked, can you leave the prototype, the printed book and the posters for someone else to install, and who minds a stand over the two public days.

**4. How do the innovation and impact criteria apply to a deliberately speculative product?**

Innovation asks whether the solution is "desirable, technologically feasible, and economically viable". Impact asks for "positive impact for Aotearoa". Compass is fictional and is meant to open a question rather than be viable.

So is the impact about the fictional product's effect on fictional New Zealand creatives, or about the film's effect on the real people who watch it?

*10 marks between the two criteria, and the wording genuinely does not resolve it.*

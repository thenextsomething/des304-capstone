# A2 mid-semester check-in, three minute talk

**Thursday 17 September, 9am. 5 marks.** Three minute oral plus the Miro board link.

Marked on one criterion: progress since the concept pitch, evidence of how you responded to previous feedback, and work in progress on the prototype and experiments.

Roughly 420 words. Do not read it. Learn the seven beats and talk.

---

**Open, 20 seconds**

Kia ora, I'm Polo, Emerging Technologies. Compass is a speculative launch film for a fictional pair of AI glasses built for creative people, set in New Zealand in 2028. Since the pitch the world is locked and the film is written, so I want to show you what changed and why.

**The Aotearoa answer, 30 seconds**

The thing I had not solved at the pitch was what makes this specifically about Aotearoa rather than a global AI story that happens to be filmed here. It is solved now, and it is solved inside the world rather than in the framing. Compass is made by a New Zealand company, Create Labs, founded by working creatives who teamed up with engineers. New Zealand is the deliberate first market, launching at home before the United States, which is the normal play for a local company going overseas. So it is a New Zealand launch film, not a global one shot in Auckland.

**The rule, 25 seconds**

The product has one rule and it got much sharper. Put Compass on and it shows you nothing. It only wakes when you are actually making something. It suggests and it assists, and it cannot create, because a human has to be making something for there to be anything to assist.

**The film, 35 seconds**

82 seconds, three creatives, intercut, mostly point of view. The first eighteen seconds are three people putting the glasses on and nothing happening. You do not know yet that that is the point. Then each of them starts working and the lens wakes. The argument is delivered by the order of the shots, so nobody has to say it, and the device never speaks in the film at all.

**Responding to feedback, 35 seconds**

Three pieces drove that. The week nine crit last semester said design the product before committing to a production approach, so the product was fully defined before a line of script was written. Navia, a photographer I spoke to, asked for a shot checklist on the lens, a way to review the last shot, and reference moodboards. All three are now real features and specific beats in the script. And Jed said use the break to fill gaps rather than plan ahead, so I audited the whole project against the briefs and answered what came out of it.

**The setback, 25 seconds**

Two weeks in August I did nothing on this. Studio work took them. The cost was specific rather than vague: the prototype route depended on one unanswered email and I let it sit three weeks. What changed is that the route no longer depends on one person, and the studio now gets told in advance which weeks it cannot have.

**The prototype, and the ask, 30 seconds**

I have changed direction here and I want your read. Rather than fabricating glasses that only ever appear on camera, I am building the whole product world: a live landing page, a printed press kit, the posters, the social presence. So someone walks up at the exhibition and it feels like a real launch, because everything a real launch has is actually there. I know the brief talks about fabrication and craftsmanship, so that is the question I need answered.

**Close, 10 seconds**

Shooting the week of the 21st. Everything is on the board.

---

## If they only remember three things

1. The world is locked and the film is written.
2. The Aotearoa question is answered inside the world, not bolted on.
3. The prototype direction has changed and needs a ruling.

## Do not

- Read it off a phone or a script. The rubric marks that directly.
- Apologise for the two quiet weeks beyond the one honest sentence. Naming a setback and what changed is worth marks. Dwelling on it is not.
- Run over. Three minutes, and they cut you off for equity.

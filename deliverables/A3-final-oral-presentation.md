## What it is

You present the final project to peers, teachers and project partners. **4 minutes to present, 2 minutes for feedback.**

## The five questions the presentation must answer

- **Why?** Why do we care, why is this a problem that needs solving. Background of the selected challenge, using research data to support the design process
- **What?** What is the proposed project or solution, what does it look like
- **Who?** Who are you, who are the stakeholders most affected or benefited, who is the target audience, why would they participate and what motivates them
- **How?** How does the proposal work, does it have steps, how would participants engage
- **Impact:** what social, cultural, economic and environmental impacts do you expect the project to have

## Deliverables

1. **Upload the slide deck.** The brief's tips: make slides highly visual, put the most important information in writing but do not make them text-intensive, do not use "Canva" type decorations, only use images that are highly pertinent to the project.
2. **Deliver the oral presentation.** The brief's tips: start by introducing yourself, do not read from your phone or from a script, use the slides as a visual aid to keep pacing but try not to read from them, face the audience and make eye contact. Partner feedback is flagged as highly valuable to learn from.
3. **Show the prototype during the presentation.** Nothing needs uploading for this part.

## Rubric, 10 marks

## What this means for Compass

This is my reading of the brief against where the project currently is, not part of the brief itself.

**Four minutes is one minute longer than A1, for a whole semester of work.** The A1 pitch was a 3-minute hard cap covering roughly the same five questions at concept level. This is the same compression problem again, with far more material and a finished film to account for.

**The film cannot be played in full.** Four minutes total, and Why, What, Who, How and Impact all have to be covered. Decide early whether to play a short section live, cut a shorter edit for the presentation, or let the poster and exhibition carry the film while the talk carries the argument. This is a real decision, not a detail.

**The prototype has to be in the room and working.** Point 3 requires showing it live. Combined with the prototype brief, which asks for a high-fidelity prototype at the final presentation and exhibition, this means the physical object needs to exist and be presentable by 21 October, eight days before the prototype's own submission deadline.

**"No Canva type decorations" is about decoration, not the tool.** The A1 deck was built in Canva and that is fine. What the brief rules out is decorative template flourish and images that are not pertinent. The Apple-launch visual language the project already uses is the right answer here anyway.

**Not reading from a script is a repeat instruction.** The same rule applied at A1, and the 28 July pitching lecture reinforced it: simple conversational language, no jargon, clearly not reading a script. The film has a script. The presentation must not.

**Consistent identity across all three A3 pieces is worth marks twice.** The visual communication criterion here asks for branding coherent with the rest of the project, and the posters and report criteria ask for the same thing. One identity system, built once, is marked in three places.

## What it is

The brief's framing: iterative prototyping is a vital part of any design and research process. It turns ideas and sketches into tangible formats that can be used for communication, testing, critique and validation.

Three statements from the brief matter most:

1. **The format should be discussed and agreed with your stream leader.** The deliverables section repeats this as "Format TBC with your stream coordinator."
2. **Even if the project focuses on an intangible outcome, such as a service or system, you are still expected to produce a physical prototype.** The brief suggests this might be a key touchpoint within the user experience, or a model that helps convey the concept, such as an architectural or interaction model.
3. **For the final presentation and Design Exhibition you are required to produce a high-fidelity prototype** representing the refined design outcome.

Prototyping is described as inherently iterative: expect multiple versions, each building on insights from the last. Begin early and plan for multiple rounds. Early versions might be low-fidelity or paper prototypes that show how the concept is being refined through self-directed research and feedback.

The visual project report must include documentation of the prototyping process, such as sketches, photos and other visuals, to communicate how the project evolved.

## Deliverables

1. **All "paper" prototypes** (or wireframes if digital), documented in the final visual project report
2. **The hi-fidelity final prototype** (digital or physical), presented live alongside the final pitch presentation

For the upload itself: a functioning QR code or link if there is one, or a specific prototype document, or images of the physical prototype.

## What this means for Compass

This is my reading of the brief against where the project currently is, not part of the brief itself.

**This is the biggest open question of the four, and it needs a conversation, not a decision made alone.** The format is explicitly "TBC with your stream coordinator". For most projects the prototype and the deliverable are the same thing. For this project they may not be: the capstone deliverable is the launch film, but the thing the film is about is a physical wearable. Three readings are possible, and the stream leader decides which one counts:

- The film is the prototype, as a diegetic prototype in Kirby's sense, which is already the project's theoretical anchor
- The Compass glasses object is the prototype, and the film is the outcome it supports
- Both, with the glasses on a plinth at the exhibition and the film playing beside them

**The brief leans toward needing a physical object regardless.** It states that a physical prototype is expected even when the outcome is intangible, and the rubric marks fabrication, materials and craftsmanship, which is language about an object rather than a video file. Half the marks sit under technical execution described as tools, materials and craftsmanship.

**That makes the appearance model non-optional.** Building a physical mock-up of the glasses has been on the horizon since Week 4 of last semester and was pushed again by the Week 9 crit, which said to design the physical product before committing to a production approach. It is already a row on the semester timeline board. This brief turns it from a good idea into 15 marks, and the industrial direction is already set: light and minimal, closest to Brilliant Labs Frame.

**It also has to survive public display.** "Carefully considered for public display" appears in the top band, and the exhibition is curated. The object gets looked at closely, by people who will pick it up.

**Paper prototypes are required, and dated ones are better.** Deliverable 1 asks for all paper prototypes or wireframes to be documented in the report. The UI work from last semester, the piano scenario, the easel experiments, the photography nudges, is exactly this material, but it needs to be presented as a documented iteration sequence rather than a folder of stills. Anything new should be dated as it happens.

**Start now, not in October.** The brief says begin prototyping early and plan for multiple rounds, and the high-fidelity version has to be in the room on 21 October for the presentation, eight days before this deadline. Working backwards from a physical object that needs fabrication, that is not a lot of runway.

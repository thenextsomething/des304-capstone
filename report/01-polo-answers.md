Polo's own answers to a full pass over every section of the report. **This is the primary
source for the report draft.** Where it disagrees with an older file in this repo, this wins.
Lightly tidied for punctuation only; the substance and the phrasing are his.

## 1. What the project is

A speculative launch video set in **2028**, when creatives in Aotearoa work differently, and
this is what that could look like. The product does not replace what they do. It adds to and
enhances their creative process.

## 2. Why he chose it

Professional practice, yes, but it is also close to him as a creative. His words:

> How do we keep creatives at the centre now that AI is just getting so good at making stuff?
> How do we still keep the craft, the taste, and all that stuff at the centre? I think there
> is beauty in creating, and AI should help you maximise your potential, not completely
> replace you in creating it.

## 3. What he was afraid of

**AI getting too good while the project was being made.**

## 4. Conversations with creatives

Every creative he has talked to is scared about AI. The fear is not that the work stops being
creative. It is: *do they even need to be in the creation process anymore, and what does that
look like for them.* The creative process is being consumed at every step, ideation, craft,
and the actual making.

## 5. Does he think AI will replace creative work

**No, and he will say so.** It is up to the creatives themselves. Still a bit unknown, but
creative work changes rather than disappears, and it moves toward creative direction.

## 6. Why glasses

An easy wearable. He does not expect people to wear them all the time. **They are for creative
work only.** And the point of the form: it sees what the user sees, through their eyes, rather
than having its own view of the world.

## 7. Why 2028

Wearables are getting really good now. A wearable he would believe in is ready next year or
the year after. 2028 is credible given how fast AI is moving.

## 8. Who an everyday creator is

> Literally just me, Chloe, and Josiah.

Photographers, videographers, piano, painting. All of it genuinely true of them. **People who
love doing creative stuff, at any level.** Professional or not does not matter.

## 9. Who it is not for

Nobody. Not deliberately excluding anyone.

## 10. What the studio gives him, and what it costs

He wants to protect his team and himself as creatives, while understanding that the new tools
matter. Keep up or get left behind. AI carries a bad stigma and it is another productivity
tool. The question he is working on in his own life as well as in the project: **how do you
position that and keep the core.**

## 11. Being on camera as the musician

**He has always wanted to play the piano and has never been good at it.** The learning curve
felt too hard and he has never had the time. He would genuinely love to get better.

That is not a casting convenience. He is playing the exact person the product is for.

## 12. Background

Filipino. The Philippines background goes in as he has written it elsewhere.

## Define

**The moment it became clear:** talking to everyone about how they felt, alongside some early
concepts he had, keeping the creative at the centre, and asking what UI features they would
want.

**"It does not create, it only assists"** he arrived at himself, out of the conversations.

**The three refusals** were not hard to commit to. He kept the rule at the centre of the whole
design.

## Develop: the compositing wall

> AI was just not good enough, so I just had to do it myself with After Effects.

The specifics: when he gave it footage, it could not do it. **Small revisions and refinements
were not possible. Consistency was not there.** So he learned After Effects himself and edited
the whole thing in After Effects himself.

## Develop: the physical prototype, the full story

This is the longest answer he gave and it is the Prototyping Process section almost whole.

1. Got a bunch of glasses.
2. **Consulted the FabLab team and people from JCar.**
3. **First draft in air-dried clay**, then oven-dried, just to see how it looked and how it
   felt.
4. He was okay with it, so he got better glasses with the frames he was looking for.
5. **Got mouldable plastic from JCar.** Moulded it around the frames, sanded it, spray-painted
   it, and added the small parts to represent where the camera and the sensors go.
6. **He has photos of both pairs of glasses.**

In his words: *a very long, hard, and tedious process.* And honestly:

> I think the first prototype I did with the clay was pretty bad, and honestly, I wasn't super
> happy with how it ended up with the physical prototype.

It was good enough to be in the film, and to base the Blender renders on, which is what it was
for.

## Develop: why the design got thicker

He was looking at other AR frames and how AR glasses actually look. It needed tech inside it
to fit powerful sensors and cameras.

> It wouldn't have been feasible for it to not have any thickness to it, because where would
> all the tech go?

## Deliver

### The fake product stack is scratched

**No website, no Instagram, no LinkedIn, no product page of any kind.** Just the poster. His reasoning:
the launch video is what he would rather have, and it is more accurate, because that is how
advanced tech products release themselves, speculative or real.

### Why a launch film, and the GPT-6 reference

> I like the GPT-6 launch video because it really made the person at the centre, and then
> GPT-6 was just a tool.

### The shoot

**Two days, not three.** Day one was the piano and the photographer. Day two was the painter.

The painter's day needed real props: **two paintings, one complete, which Polo painted himself,
and one half-finished version of the same painting.**

It went well. Nothing was changed on the day. They stuck to the storyboard, got a lot of
B-roll, everyone was well briefed and understood the prop, and it ran very smoothly.

## Impact

**He could not make the impact planning workshop on 22 September, but did some work on it
separately.**

### For a creative person in Auckland

They still feel safe and secure in their own creative practice. AI as a real tool of assistance
rather than a replacement. **Beginner or professional, it does not matter, it helps either way.**

### The wider impact, which is the bigger claim

> I would like this product to be what creatives see and look at to set the example for other
> tech products, and anything AI or anything tech that will assist creatives. Our philosophy is
> the creatives' philosophy, and that should be representative of all products for creatives.

Plus other product lines beyond this one. The wider impact is the philosophy spreading, not the
device selling.

### The honest limits

- **It has nothing to do with the environment.** Say so.
- **There is a cost barrier**, a barrier to entry.
- The philosophy only works if other companies carry it too.

### The negative impact

**Intellectual property, and the fact that it sees what you are doing.** His position: not
necessarily negative if it is stored and secured in the right ways. This is the direct bridge
into kaitiakitanga.

good. And start learning After Effects sooner, because it took longer than expected.

**What he learned without expecting to:** generating mock storyboard frames and mock storyboard
videos using AI.

**Real next steps:** refine the actual product, then use real AR glasses and work out how the
interface looks in real life.

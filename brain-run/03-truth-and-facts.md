## This step changes job, and here is exactly how

The step as written exists to stop us claiming a product does something it
cannot do yet. Its whole machinery is built around one distinction: shipped
versus in production versus roadmap, founder-confirmed, with the honesty line
on top. **Compass does not exist. Nothing about it is shipped, everything about
it is invented, and the distinction the step is built on is empty here.**

Deleting the step would be the wrong answer, because the failure it prevents
is alive in a different form. A speculative launch film can lie in two ways,
and both are expensive at exactly the same moment the original failure was
expensive - after the shoot, in the report, in front of a marker.

**So the gate keeps its name, its stamp and its authority, and swaps its two
questions:**

| The step normally asks | For Compass it asks |
|---|---|
| Is this feature shipped today? | **Is the fiction internally consistent?** Does anything in the world, the product, the interface or the film contradict anything else in the record? A speculative product that contradicts itself stops being a diegetic prototype and becomes a student mistake, and a marker will find the seam faster than a customer would. |
| Is this number real and permissioned? | **Is every real-world statistic stated at the strength its source actually supports?** Unchanged in substance. This half of the step is doing its original job, because the numbers in this project are real even though the product is not, and this is the only place in the whole film where a genuine honesty failure is possible. |

**Three things carry over unchanged.**

1. **The gate still holds step 7.** No script is written or rewritten against
   PENDING rows. That refusal is what makes the step real rather than
   aspirational (framework, the gate table).
2. **RS-INV-02 still applies, redefined.** Nothing enters the inventory
   ungated. Here the gate is "is it ruled and pinned in
   `compass-product-definition.md`", not "is it live today".
3. **The honesty line still gets signed once**, rewritten below, so it can
   never ping-pong.

**What was adapted and why.** There is no client founder to send a check to,
so the "two-minute founder check" becomes a self-check addressed to Polo as
the only person who can rule any of it (section 5). The engine still drafts
and stops; the director still stamps. And the fast-track (founder-ruled
2026-08-24) is worth naming as available: the posters need finished hero
frames by 15 October, so if the shoot has to happen before every row below is
ruled, the fast-track's rule applies - anything unconfirmed ships only
future-framed, and the full sheet still closes before the edit locks.

## 3. Internal consistency of the fiction

_Fifteen rows. Each is a place the record disagrees with itself or leaves a
load-bearing thing undecided. "Blocking" means a shot cannot be designed until
it is ruled. Statuses: **contradiction** (two live documents say different
things), **undecided** (nobody has ruled), **exposure** (consistent, but a
marker or viewer will push on it)._

### The ones that block a shot

**A1. The reference layer is in two places at once. CONTRADICTION. Blocking.**
The product definition, rewritten 16 September, puts the reference images and
moodboard **top left**, "so the thing he's working toward sits where he looks
first". Two live board frames still put it **along the bottom edge**: "The
reference layer, along the bottom edge" (the Navia frame) and, worse, the
hardware-landscape frame builds a whole argument on it - "Compass keeps three
of those and breaks one knowingly. The bottom edge goes to the reference layer
instead, because the convention puts directional cues down there and Compass
has nowhere to steer you."
**Why this one matters more than a stale note:** the 16 September move did not
just relocate a panel, it **silently killed the only recorded argument for how
Compass breaks the category's layout convention**. The product definition
flags that the Even Realities argument died that day; nobody noticed this one
died too. Either the bottom edge is now empty and the convention-breaking
claim is simply "Compass puts nothing where everyone else puts directional
cues" (which is a better argument, not a worse one), or the layer moves back.
**Recommendation:** keep the top-left layout, rewrite the claim as "Compass
leaves the bottom edge empty, because it has nowhere to steer you", and
correct both board frames. Needs Polo's ruling.

**A2. The "calm and quiet" argument does not survive the panels.
CONTRADICTION. Blocking the report, not the shoot.**
The hardware frame still reads: "Compass is warm white with no borders, mostly
type, mostly absent, and it fades rather than switching." As of 16 September
Compass is translucent glass panels with thin light edges, which is borders,
and it is denser and more legible. The product definition already admits "the
change moves Compass toward the kind of dense panelled readout the old argument
was defined against rather than away from it" and says there is no replacement
argument yet.
**Recommendation, and it is already half-written in the record:** the new
argument rests on **when Compass appears and what it refuses to do**, not on
how little it draws. That is the director's own steer in the product
definition. It is a stronger argument than the old one, because restraint in
time is harder to copy than restraint in pixels. Needs writing and ruling
before the report.

**A3. Does the empty centre survive the illustrator looking down?
UNDECIDED. Blocking.**
The rule is absolute: "Nothing in the centre of your vision. Ever." The
illustrator's lens has guide lines "over the page" and proportion corrections
"called out on the drawing itself". When she is drawing she is looking down,
so the page **is** the centre of her vision. The board's own lens frame
resolves this if you read it carefully, because it marks a band "below here is
the working band, where your hands are" separate from the reserved centre. But
the rule as written in the product definition does not carry that distinction,
and the film is 60 to 70 per cent point of view, which means the audience sees
exactly this.
**Recommendation:** rewrite the rule as "nothing is ever drawn in the centre of
the lens; Compass only marks the working band, where your hands already are."
That keeps the absolute and makes it shootable. Needs Polo's ruling.

**A4. "Corrections" is an instruction, and Compass is supposed to whisper.
CONTRADICTION. Blocking.**
The illustrator's lens carries "Corrections to the body proportions and the
face proportions, called out on the drawing itself." The DES303 record says
"Compass suggestions are framed as whispers, not instructions" and "It prompts,
it does not perform." A correction called out on the drawing is a machine
marking your work wrong. It is the single most replacement-flavoured element in
the whole interface, and it sits on the character whose slot exists precisely
because "illustrators are the most recognisable face of the AI replacement
fear".
**Recommendation:** reframe as observation rather than verdict. Compass shows
the reference proportion beside her line rather than marking her line wrong.
Same information, opposite posture, and it costs one word in the product
definition. Needs Polo's ruling.

**A5. Who wrote the shot checklist? UNDECIDED. Blocking.**
The photographer's checklist shows "five items with three of them ticked". If
Compass generated the shot list, Compass created something, and the one rule
breaks. If the checklist came from the brief and Compass only tracks it, the
rule holds and the feature is better for it, because tracking is assistance in
its purest form.
**Recommendation:** the checklist is the client's brief, loaded in, and Compass
only ticks. One line in the product definition, and it turns Navia's request
into a proof of the rule rather than a hole in it. Needs Polo's ruling.

**A6. "It never shows you someone else's finished version of what you are
attempting" versus the illustrator's reference. CONTRADICTION. Blocking.**
The never-do list forbids exactly that. The illustrator's lens carries "A
reference of what she is actually trying to make, so the thing itself rather
than a mood." If she is drawing a figure and Compass shows her a finished
illustration of that figure, the never-do is broken on screen.
**Recommendation:** the reference is a source photograph, a posing reference or
her own earlier work, never a finished illustration of the same subject by
someone else. That is almost certainly what was meant, and it needs to be
written down because the shot will be built from the words. Needs Polo's
ruling.

### The ones that change what gets designed

**A7. One interface language versus the customisable-per-wearer rule.
UNDECIDED since 12 September, self-flagged.**
The record says so itself: "the customisable-per-wearer rule still sits against
a film needing one recognisable visual language across three intercut scenes."
The 16 September change actually solves most of this, because the glass-panel
language is now shared and only the contents differ, and the board says the
photographer's lens is "the model the other two now follow".
**Recommendation:** rule it resolved and write it down. The language is fixed
and the slots are customisable, which is exactly what the board already says:
"The lens is a set of allowed slots rather than a fixed screen." Cheap, and it
closes an open item.

**A8. The temple timing pulse was cut, and it was carrying an argument.
EXPOSURE, self-flagged.**
The board: "the timing pulse felt through the temple is gone as of 16
September, which leaves the argument against a heads-up display without the
single feature that used to carry it." Combined with A2, two of the arguments
that separated Compass from a heads-up display died on the same day.
**Recommendation:** accept the loss and do not restore a feature to save an
argument. The replacement is A2's: the separation is the empty state, not the
rendering. But it must be written, because right now the project has a hole
where its differentiation argument used to be.

**A9. The light reading has nowhere to go. UNDECIDED, self-flagged.**
The product definition: "I have not decided yet whether it survives as its own
element somewhere else on the lens or whether it just folds into the settings
panel." It is one of the four things Navia actually asked for, and the board
notes it is the only one with no shot.
**Recommendation:** fold it into the settings panel and say so. It is a camera
setting, it belongs with the camera settings, and "all four of her requests are
in" then survives as a true sentence.

**A10. The display spec is technically incoherent. CONTRADICTION.**
The fact sheet says "Display: Binocular waveguide, monochrome, full colour on
the reference layer". A monochrome display cannot show full colour. This is
printed on the press kit and goes on the exhibition table, where it is exactly
the kind of line a technically minded visitor reads twice.
**Recommendation:** "Binocular waveguide, full colour. Used monochromatically
except on the reference layer." Same design intent, and it stands up.

**A11. The company-name resolution has no home. UNDECIDED. Blocking the
landing page.**
The world-building file is explicit that "a company called Create Labs needs
its reading made explicit somewhere ... Left unstated, a marker can just as
easily read it as a contradiction the student did not notice." The resolving
line exists. But the actors "speak about their day rather than about the
product", so it cannot be dialogue, and the record only ever says "one line on
the landing page or one beat in the film".
**Recommendation:** make it the film's end card and the landing-page headline,
both. It is the best sentence in the project, it resolves the name, and as an
ending it is exactly the structure FP-22 describes - the identity lands last,
after the proof.

**A12. Compass needs a camera, and the board says it left the camera behind.
CONTRADICTION, resolvable.**
The hardware frame: "Compass takes the disappearing and leaves the camera
behind." Compass sees what you are working on, shows the last photo taken, and
reads a page well enough to place guide lines. It cannot do any of that without
sensors looking outward.
**Recommendation:** the line means Compass leaves behind the camera *as a
capture product* - it is not for recording other people - and the answer to
"but it can see" is already the strongest thing in the project: everything is
processed on the device and nothing leaves. Rewrite the board line, and let the
privacy rule do the work. This is an upgrade, not a patch.

**A13. Compass never speaks, but the musician has a metronome. UNDECIDED.**
The never-do list says "It never speaks. Compass has no voice, in the film or
in the product." It does not say Compass makes no sound. The metronome is
described as "visible on the lens, because the film has audio and the timing
has to be watchable as well as audible" - which implies the timing is audible,
and if Compass is producing that sound then Compass has an audio output.
**Recommendation:** the metronome is visual only and the audible timing comes
from the room, the piano or the music bed. Cleanest, keeps the never-do
absolute, and costs nothing on screen.

**A14. 38 grams is heavier than the 2025 product it is positioned against.
EXPOSURE.**
Even Realities G2 is 36 grams today, in magnesium and titanium, and the record
calls it "the closest existing thing to the thin direction". Compass is 38
grams in 2028 and is described as "thin and light". A visitor who knows the
category will notice the speculative future product is heavier than the real
present one.
**Recommendation:** either go to 32 to 34 grams, or keep 38 and own it in the
fact sheet - Compass carries a binocular colour display and prescription-ready
light-reactive lenses, and G2 has no camera and no speakers. The second is
better and more honest, but it has to be said rather than left for the visitor
to work out.

**A15. The runtime is ruled as a band and asserted as a number. CONTRADICTION,
trivial to close.**
`world-building.md` says 70 to 90 seconds. A board frame asserts 82 seconds in
its own title. Nothing is wrong, but step 4 cannot lock a band.
**Recommendation:** lock the number the board already carries and move on.

### 4b. What else will not stand up

**B1. The 65% has the wrong base written next to it.** The board says
"Verian for Manatū Taonga, n = 2,000 NZ adults, Sept to Oct 2025" beside a
figure described as "of New Zealand creators who use digital tools". Those are
two different denominators. The 65% is a subgroup of the 2,000, and the
subgroup's own size is not recorded anywhere in the project. **This is the same
class of error as the USC geography one, and it has not been caught.** It needs
the subgroup named and, ideally, its n. Also: `research/references.md` cites
only Manatū Taonga as the publisher and never names Verian as the research
agency, so the citation and the board disagree about who collected it.

**B2. The AI Forum numbers are about all industries, not creative ones.**
"AI had created new roles in 55 per cent of organisations while 14 per cent put
job losses down to it" comes from a survey of 166 New Zealand **business
decision-makers**. It is used on a frame arguing about creative work. It is a
fair piece of national context and an unfair piece of creative-sector evidence.
Same family as the USC problem. **Recommendation:** keep it, label it "across
all industries", and never let it sit in a sentence about creatives without
that label.

**C1. Seven citations cannot currently be stood up at all.**
`research/references.md` marks them itself: The Spinoff (2025) plus six trade
press items - ABC News on Google Glass, CNBC on Glass Enterprise, Fast Company
on Friend's campaign, HUDS+GUIS on Microsoft 2011, Laptop Mag on the Humane Ai
Pin, and Quartz on the Meta stage demo. All were taken from secondary
reporting rather than primary pages and all need a byline off the live page.
Two board claims rest directly on two of them (see C3 and C4), which means
they are not just incomplete citations, they are unsupported assertions on a
graded frame.

**C2. One citation needs a date check:** the Apple Vision Pro newsroom item,
marked NEEDS CHECK in the references file.

**C3. "Humane ... shut down inside fourteen months" does not compute from the
project's own sources.** `research/references.md` dates the Ai Pin
announcement 9 November 2023 (TechCrunch, Heater) and the asset sale 18
February 2025 (TechCrunch). That is fifteen months and nine days. From the
April 2024 ship it is about ten months. Fourteen is neither.
**Recommendation:** say "about fifteen months from announcement to shutdown",
which is both true and, if anything, a stronger line.

**C4. "the demo failed twice in front of the room"** rests on Quartz, which has
no byline and which the references file itself notes has no official clip. The
"twice" is unsourced precision. **Recommendation:** "the demo failed on stage"
unless a primary source turns up.

**C5. "New Yorkers wrote over Friend's subway posters within days"** rests on
Fast Company, byline missing, and "within days" is unsourced precision on top
of an unsourced source.

**C6. "Six weeks and US$60k for a keynote"** on Knowledge Navigator is stated
as fact on the precedent frame and has no source anywhere in the project. It is
a widely repeated figure, which is exactly the condition under which a number
is most likely to be folklore. **Either source it or drop it.**

**C7. "Soul Machines came out of the University of Auckland in 2016"** has no
citation in the project. The references file dates BabyX to 2014 and does not
date the company. Probably correct, currently unsupported.

**C8. The 76% Māori music creators figure has two possible sources of
record.** The board attributes it to "APRA AMCOS and Goldmedia, AI and Music,
August 2024, via Taiuru and Associates"; the references file lists APRA AMCOS
(2024) and Taiuru (2024) as separate entries. One of them is the source and the
other is the route. Pick one, cite the other as "as reported in". The handling
of the base itself is exemplary and should not be touched: "Drawn as 53, not
scaled to 100, because the base is small and hiding that would be worse than
showing it."

**C9. The Massey study must always be called a pilot.** The source title says
so and the board is already honest about the evidence around it ("a handful of
named creatives and ten interviews, which are real but they aren't
measurement"). Keep that honesty in the report, where it is worth marks rather
than costing them.

### 4c. Two honesty items that are not statistics

**D1. The bad camera test.** The journal already caught this and it belongs in
this gate rather than in a note: the Capstone HQ page said "let's say that we
did do these tests". Nothing gets claimed that did not happen. The honest
version is that the break's After Effects experiments were the tests, and they
get documented as what they actually were. **This is the only place in the
whole project where an academic-integrity problem was actively forming, and it
was caught. Keep it caught.**

**D2. The AI-use log.** Every item is Lane 2: AI use is permitted and must be
acknowledged with tool, prompt and date, and the acknowledgement was ruled to
land in the visual project report. The running log is referenced in the record
as a standing habit. **Nothing in the repo shows it exists.** Every previs
prompt, every Claude session and this pipeline run itself are all loggable
events. If the log is not being kept, the cheapest possible fix is to start it
now and backfill from the journal and the git history, which both carry dates.

### 4d. What passed, and it is worth saying

The link provenance work on the precedent set is the strongest piece of
verification in the project and should be left alone: every video id checked
against YouTube's and Vimeo's own metadata on 15 September, thirteen of fifteen
confirmed as the maker's own upload, and the two that cannot be are explained
rather than hidden. The Planet City date, the non-existent 2013 Microsoft
instalment and the Cluely film mix-up were all found and corrected. That is the
standard the rest of section 4 should be brought up to.

# Call with Josiah, Tuesday 15 September

He's a creative director and director, and the reason for the call is casting. Casting is the smallest thing he could give you.

---

## What to bring

The script (`concept-notes/film-script-v1.md`), and the one-line version of the product: put them on and they show you nothing, they only wake when you're actually making something, and they can't create.

## The five things to get out of it, in order

**1. The three wearers.** A musician, a photographer and videographer merged into one, and an illustrator. Varied in age, gender and background so the trio doesn't read as three versions of the same person. Course rules mean friends and family only, so what you need from Josiah is names he can vouch for rather than a casting call.

**2. His availability for the week of 21 September.** This is the thing to ask early rather than at the end. Three half days, three different rooms. If he can direct or run one of them, you're behind the camera instead of doing both jobs, and on the four locked-off tracked setups that's the difference between getting them clean and getting them nearly.

**3. Someone on stills.** The posters need real photographs at 300 dpi and a 4K video frame across an A1 only lands at about half that, so the stills camera has to run alongside the video. Doing both yourself on a locked setup is how one of them ends up worse. If Josiah can shoot stills while you shoot video, that solves 5 of the poster's 10 marks in one move.

**4. How he'd shoot the point of view.** Two thirds of this film is first person and that's the fiddly part, so head-mounted rig versus handheld at eyeline, how much of the hands to hold in frame, how to keep it from reading like a body-cam. He'll have opinions and they'll be worth more than another week of you working it out.

**5. Locations.** A bedroom with a keyboard, a garage or small studio, and a desk by a window. All Auckland, all real interiors.

## The one that isn't about production at all

**Show him the opening and find out whether it lands.**

The film's whole argument rests on the first eighteen seconds, where three people put the glasses on and nothing happens, and the audience isn't supposed to know yet that the nothing is the point. If that doesn't land, the film doesn't work and everything after it is decoration.

Josiah is exactly the right person to test it on, because he reads film for a living and he doesn't already know the concept the way you do. Describe the opening, don't explain what it's doing, and then ask him what he thinks is going on.

Two reasons this is worth more than it sounds. If it doesn't land you find out now rather than in the edit, and either way it's real feedback from a real person which goes straight into the feedback log on the board, and the report has a criterion asking for exactly that kind of commentary alongside the work.

Course rules note: this is an informal conversation with someone you know, which is allowed. It isn't a formal interview or a test with the public, and it shouldn't be written up as one.

## After the call

Log it. Who said what, what you changed because of it, and what you didn't. Straight into the feedback log and the week 7 frame while it's fresh.

## Corrections to numbers already used

**APRA AMCOS 82 per cent.** The A1 deck says creators "feared AI could end their ability to make a living". The published wording is "are concerned that the use of AI in music could lead to music creators no longer being able to make a living from their work". Use the published wording. The 38 per cent already using AI is accurate. Footnote that the sample is APRA AMCOS members across Australia, New Zealand and members living overseas. The deck is submitted and can't be changed, so in the report this is a disclosure rather than a correction, and the stronger wording never gets reprinted.

**USC LABarometer 59 per cent.** It is Los Angeles County entertainment workers, not the United States generally. Say so every time the number appears, including on a poster where there's no room for a footnote, which is why it's now in the label and on both bars of the chart instead of only in the small print.

**Creative New Zealand median income.** $19,500 from creative work alone. The $15,000 figure is specifically Deaf and disabled artists and $16,500 is female creative professionals, so $15,000 is not the headline number.

**Manatū Taonga 65 per cent, caught 17 September.** The Aotearoa frame had "n = 2,000 NZ adults" printed next to a figure that's actually a subgroup of those 2,000, the creators who use digital tools, which are two different denominators sitting side by side. Same class of error as the USC geography one. The frame now says the base is the subgroup and that the subgroup's own n isn't published anywhere I can find, and the 2,000 is labelled as the whole survey. Verian is named as the agency here as well as on the frame now.

**AI Forum 55 and 14 per cent, caught 17 September.** They're from 166 New Zealand business decision-makers across all industries, and they were sitting on a frame arguing about creative work specifically, which makes them fair national context and unfair creative-sector evidence. Kept, labelled "across all industries" on the frame, and they never go in a sentence about creatives without that label.

**The 76 per cent Māori figure had two sources of record, caught 17 September.** The frame ran "APRA AMCOS and Goldmedia, AI and Music, August 2024, via Taiuru and Associates" as if that were one citation. Taiuru is now the source of record because that's where the Māori breakdown is reported, with the APRA AMCOS and Goldmedia study named as the data underneath it. The handling of the base itself was already right and hasn't been touched: drawn as 40 of 53, not scaled to 100, because the base is small and hiding it would be worse than showing it.

**"Humane shut down inside fourteen months", cut 23 September.** Doesn't compute off this file. Announcement 9 November 2023, asset sale 18 February 2025, which is fifteen months and nine days, and ten months from the April 2024 ship. Replace with "about fifteen months from announcement to shutdown".

**"The demo failed twice", cut back to "the demo failed on stage", 23 September.** The source has no byline and there's no official clip, so the count is unsupported precision.

**"New Yorkers wrote over the posters within days", timing cut 23 September.** Same problem, unsourced precision on top of a source that still needs a byline.

**"Six weeks and US$60k" for Knowledge Navigator.** No source anywhere in the project. Either it gets a primary source or it comes out, and the default is out.

**"Soul Machines came out of the University of Auckland in 2016".** No citation in the project. This file dates BabyX to 2014 and doesn't date the company.

**"Stats NZ has discontinued the Screen Industry Survey"**, on the Aotearoa frame. No citation in the project at all. It needs a Stats NZ page behind it or it comes off the frame, and it's flagged on the frame itself in the meantime.

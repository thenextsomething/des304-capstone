### The divergence every card inherits, stated once

**PROBLEM is absent from all four, and that is a break with SP-LAUNCH-01, not a
reading of it.** The rule's spine runs THE HOOK -> ENCAPSULATE -> PROBLEM ->
MAGIC -> CLOSE, and its only sanctioned collapse is when the pain IS the hook,
which merges HOOK and PROBLEM rather than deleting PROBLEM. Here the pain is
deleted, because the register was ruled to have no struggle beat and no failure
beat, and because the before rule is minimal to zero on a viewer who is already
living it (VG-AWARE-03, profile block 1).

So every card below runs a five-segment spine with a hole where the fourth rule
of the launch branch puts its villain, and every card has to answer the same
question differently: what carries the negative when the negative cannot be
staged. FP-21 says the viewer must feel the imbalance before the resolution
means anything, and the four cards are, more than anything else, four different
answers to that.

I am flagging this once here rather than four times below, because it is a
property of the project's own rulings and not a property of any concept. It
goes into step 6 as a set-level flag.

### The second set-level tension, and how I resolved it

The brief's section B says the open is already decided ("a person puts the
glasses on, and nothing happens") and quotes the record's ruling that "the
opening scenes are third person, to introduce the characters and to show them
putting the glasses on". The brief's section E then makes the open the axis the
four concepts vary along. Both cannot be true, so I had to pick a reading.

**The reading I took:** section E's written fixed list is the authority, because
CP-SPREAD-03 exists precisely to make that list the thing generation honours,
and that list says "60 to 70 per cent point of view with third-person openings"
and stops there. So I have held third-person openings as fixed and treated
"shows them putting the glasses on" as part of what varies. Cards B, C and D all
use that permission and each one says so in its dependencies.

If Polo rules the other way and the putting-on ritual is fixed too, then B, C and
D all need rebuilding and A is the only survivor of the four. That is worth
knowing before he reads them.

## Card A: Nothing happens

**What the film is, in one line.** A person puts on a pair of AI glasses and the
lens stays empty, and the film is the twelve seconds you spend waiting for it to
fill and the seventy you spend realising it never will until you start.

**The feel** - patient, then relieved. Like watching someone hold a door open
for a long time and only working out at the end who it was for.

**The open, specifically.** Third person, one static wide of the musician at the
piano. She picks Compass up off the lid, puts it on, settles, and the lens is
empty. A single unbroken slow push-in, no cut, holds on that for about eleven
seconds while nothing arrives. The only motion in the frame is her, and she is
not doing anything yet either. The audience's expectation is doing all the work,
because in this category the lens fills the instant the device goes on and
everyone watching has seen that happen a hundred times.

This is the pack's own recommendation, and it has a precedent inside the brain
that matches the register exactly: the `openai-apple` reference set's take on
Apple's "Great Ideas Start On Mac" (CV-0037) is **hold on literal nothing and
let it earn the payoff**, and that film runs one unbroken 42-second push-in on a
blank laptop screen before the frame erupts into 29 cuts in its last thirteen.
This is that open at a quarter of the length.

**How it runs across the three wearers.** The binding device is **the wake**:
the same event happening three times at three crafts. The first key, the first
frame, the first mark, and the lens arriving a beat behind each one. The film is
not three stories running in parallel, it is one moment told three ways, and the
cuts between wearers land on the wake itself so the rhyme is unmissable. Musician
first because she carries the open and the hardest composite. Photographer
second, because her four panels are the widest the lens ever gets and she is the
proof that a busy lens still leaves the centre clear. Illustrator third, because
her guide lines vanishing is the tell-a-friend moment and it belongs late.

**What it does with the one rule.** It makes the rule the film's opening
argument and its structural engine at the same time. Nothing in this film moves
before a human moves, and the audience learns that by being made to wait for it
three times. FP-23's test is passed more directly here than in any other card:
every segment traces to the one truth because the one truth IS the cut logic.

**The hook** - **no existing card matches this.** The library is open by founder
ruling 2026-08-01, so a no-match is a card candidate rather than a fault, and I
am logging it as one: **HP-hold-on-nothing**, the withheld interface. Open on a
device that is visibly on and visibly doing nothing, and hold past the point the
category has trained the viewer to expect the fill. Adjacent to HP-scene-cold-open
(active, five sightings, all `skit` rows) because nobody addresses the camera and
the product arrives inside the scene, but the distinctive move is the hold itself
and scene-cold-open does not describe it. Precedent: CV-0037. Candidate goes to
step 6's choice log.

**Archetype and material** - `skit`, shoot-led, `openai-apple` register worn as a
tone. Carried by people (RS-PEOPLE-01: one willing human puts the shoot-led family
on the table whatever else is missing) and places (three real rooms, unaltered).
RS-PRODUCT-04 fires and is stated rather than hidden: there is no demoable product,
which is a routing fact and not a gap, and it changes what the video is made of.

**What it costs to be true.** Seven composited interface shots - three wakes, the
lane, the four panels, the guide lines, the vanish - so it sits comfortably under
the eight-shot cap with one spare, which is the most headroom of the four. One
shoot day, one location dressed three times, which is what PF-07 requires without
an exception call. PF-08 does not fire: the glasses are a real prop and the
interface is 2D compositing, so no hardware that does not exist is being modelled.
PF-04 fails on the ledger, as it does for all four, because `skit` is the most
expensive of the ten entries and the semester budget is NZD 1,000 to 1,500; PF-05
is why it proceeds anyway, and the imputed day rate gets written down next to the
decision. Every number in this paragraph is an estimate against a ledger where
nearly every entry is tagged as one (PF-06).

**The 15-second loop** - cut from MAGIC 1 and 2, not from the open. That matters
and it is the fix for this card's biggest weakness, because AL-WEB-02 says the
loop is cut from the film's most visual segments rather than from its first
fifteen seconds, so the loop can be all lens and all motion while the film itself
still opens on nothing.

**Rules it satisfies, by id.** FP-04 (pattern-interrupt against the category norm,

CP-LAUNCH-05 (the base concept lands in the first seconds, carried by the object
rather than by a line) · VG-AWARE-04 (the slot exists and is crowded, so the hook
differentiates) · RS-CORE-02 (the rooms, the piano and the sketchbook are all real
and only the lens is built).

**Rules it breaks, by id, knowingly.** SP-LAUNCH-01 (PROBLEM deleted, not collapsed
- the set-level divergence) · FP-21 (the negative is never made real, and this card
has the weakest answer of the four, because the absence it holds on has nothing to
be measured against) · FP-14 (the villain never appears, and here it is not even
implied by a contrast) · AL-ALL-03 in spirit (the exhibition screen gives one glance
and this film spends eleven seconds before anything happens; the loop deliberately
does not inherit this, which is the mitigation, but the film itself still does) ·
FP-20's non-event test is arguable on the hook, because a segment where nothing
changes is the definition of a non-event segment and this card's whole argument is
that the non-event IS the event.

**Which of the unruled eight it depends on** - items 2 (the villain that never
appears), 3 (the identity close), 4 (82 seconds), 5 (one shoot day), 6 (the
eight-shot cap), 7 (the loop), 8 (the word ceiling). It does not depend on item 1,
because it plays the same either way. It is the only card of the four that does not
also depend on the putting-on ritual being negotiable, since it uses it.

**Self-critique, honest.** Its whole argument rests on the audience noticing an
absence, and the failure mode is that they read it as a device that has not booted
yet rather than a device that is choosing to wait. The viewer cannot tell
deliberate emptiness from a black screen, because both look identical, and nothing
in the first eleven seconds tells them which one they are watching. The brief half
admits this when it says the film "cannot rely on restraint in rendering" and has to
be "restraint in timing, shown" - but restraint in timing only reads if the viewer
has seen the alternative, and this card never shows them the alternative. That is
the deepest problem with it and it is structural rather than executional. Second and
smaller: at an exhibition, in a noisy room, with a visitor who decides in two
seconds, eleven seconds of a woman sitting still is the single hardest thing in the
set to survive, and the loop fix rescues the loop without rescuing the film.

## Card B: Three hands

**What the film is, in one line.** A film about three pairs of hands making three
things, cut so tightly that they read as one pair, and the glasses do not appear
until you have already decided you are watching people work.

**The feel** - fast, tactile, close. Like a cooking film where you never see a
face, except what is being made is a phrase, a photograph and a drawing.

**The open, specifically.** No faces, no glasses, no interface. Frame one is a
macro of a hand on a piano key, one second. Cut: a thumb on a shutter, one second.
Cut: a pencil finding the paper, one second. Three hands, three rooms, three first
actions, in the first four seconds, and then all three lenses wake at once across a
three-way cut and the glasses are revealed on the fourth beat. The device is
introduced by its effect and only afterwards by itself, which is the exact inverse
of the category's own grammar, where the device goes on first and the world arrives
second.

This is HP-scene-cold-open executed at speed. It also takes the `openai-apple`
reference set's measured signature rather than its stated one: the brain's own
measurement across that set (2026-08-04, frames and cuts) is that **the signature
rhythm is contrast, not slowness**, and that the patience is what buys the burst.
Card A takes the patience half. This card takes the burst half and puts the patience
after it, which is a genuinely different reading of the same reference.

**How it runs across the three wearers.** The binding device is **the hands**, and
it is a grammar rather than an event. Every single transition in the film is a hand
to a hand: a hand on a key becomes a hand on a shutter becomes a hand on a pencil,
matched on position, direction and speed so the three rooms read as one continuous
gesture. Nobody ever meets, and the edit insists they are doing the same thing
anyway. Compass is the thing that watches hands, so the film's cut logic and the
product's logic are the same logic.

**What it does with the one rule.** It makes the rule unbreakable at the level of
the edit rather than at the level of a beat. This film structurally cannot show
Compass doing anything before a hand does something, because hands are what the cuts
are made of, so every interface frame in the film arrives inside a shot that a hand
has already started. The rule is not stated, demonstrated or argued anywhere in the
film. It is just never violated, and the viewer absorbs it without being told.

**The hook** - **HP-scene-cold-open** (active, founder-ruled 2026-08-05). The
viewer is dropped into something already running, nobody addresses the camera, and
the product arrives inside the scene. Its native home is `skit`-archetype videos,
which is the archetype on the record, so this is the cleanest card match of the four
and the only one where the selected archetype's own opening pattern is also the
selected hook. The card's minting rider applies and passes: the pattern is not being
forced onto a film it does not fit.

**Archetype and material** - `skit`, shoot-led, `openai-apple` register. Carried by
people and places, same as A. The macro work is the one real production difference:
this card wants a macro lens and three very controlled hand setups, which is a
shopping-list problem rather than a scheduling one.

**What it costs to be true.** Seven composited interface shots, same count as A,

**The 15-second loop** - this is the card that wins the loop outright. Its first
fifteen seconds ARE the loop, already, with no cutting down: three hands, three
wakes, three faces, and it plays silent, it plays on a loop without a seam, and it
stops a passer-by from across a room. Of the four, this is the only one where the
film and the loop want the same fifteen seconds.

**Flags** - same truth flags as A (`[UNCONFIRMED: A1, A3, A5, A6]`), plus one this
card owns alone: it depends on the putting-on ritual being negotiable, because there

**Rules it satisfies, by id.** FP-03 (the hook earns the next five seconds with one

segment.

**Which of the unruled eight it depends on** - items 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, same as A,
plus the putting-on ritual being negotiable, which is not one of the eight and is the
set-level tension I flagged in section 1.

**Self-critique, honest.** It is the best-made film in the set and the worst
argument. Everything that makes it watchable is craft, and the concept underneath it
is thinner than the other three, because "the hands are the binding" is a style
decision dressed as a structural one. Strip the match cuts out and there is no film
left, which is the test CP-LAUNCH-01 is really applying when it says a launch leads
with the villain, the vision or a wow statement - this one leads with an edit.
Second, and this is the one that would make a director reject it: it is the only card
where the marker's read gets genuinely worse, because a report has to explain in
words what the film does, and "the binding device is a match cut" is a sentence that
reads as technique rather than argument in a document graded on process.

## Card C: The stop

**What the film is, in one line.** It opens as exactly the film every other company
in this category would make, a lens full of beautiful information, and then all three
people stop working at the same moment and every lens goes empty, and that is the
product.

**The feel** - dense, then suddenly quiet. Like a room where the music cuts out and
you hear that everybody has been talking loudly.

**The open, specifically.** Frame one is inside the lens. Point of view, the
photographer, mid-shoot, and the lens is at its absolute maximum: the reference and
moodboard top left, the shot checklist top right, the last frame she took on the
right with its signal blue edge, the settings panel left. Four panels, all live, all
moving, and a completely empty centre. Held for about eight seconds while she works,
so the audience gets to enjoy it, and gets to think they know what film this is.

The empty centre is present from frame one, so the argument is being made before the
viewer knows an argument is being made, and that is the thing that keeps this open on
the right side of the anti-brief. It is Compass at full stretch, not a parody of
somebody else's lens, and that distinction is the difference between a diegetic
prototype and an essay.

**How it runs across the three wearers.** The binding device is **the stop**, and it
binds at the end rather than at the beginning. The film runs full for fifty seconds,
cutting between three people deep in three crafts with three busy lenses, and then at
about second fifty-two all three of them stop at once. Hands lift off the keys, off
the camera, off the page, on the same cut, and every lens empties on the same frame.
The tell-a-friend moment stops being a detail in the illustrator's scene and becomes
the film's climax, run three times simultaneously.

This is the exact inversion of card A. A runs empty to full and its subject is
arrival. C runs full to empty and its subject is departure.

**What it does with the one rule.** It proves the rule by removing the product
rather than by withholding it, which is the only reading in the set where the viewer
gets to feel what the rule costs. You watch fifty seconds of a lens being genuinely
useful, you get attached to it, and then it leaves the instant the work stops, and
the point lands as a small loss rather than as a claim. That is the closest anything
in this set comes to FP-21's requirement that the viewer feel the imbalance, because
here the imbalance is manufactured out of the product's own generosity rather than
out of anybody's struggle.

## Card D: What it won't do

**What the film is, in one line.** Three times, Compass gets to the exact edge of
doing the work for somebody and stops, and the film is about the gap it leaves.

**The feel** - close, deliberate, slightly held-breath. Like watching someone hand
you a tool rather than take the job off you.

**The open, specifically.** Third person, static, the musician at the piano. She
already has Compass on when the film starts, we never see her put it on, and the lens
is empty while her hands are already moving fast. Held about seven seconds. The
image is a machine that is switched on and idle while a human works hard, which is
the precise inverse of every image this category produces, and the contradiction is
doing the hooking rather than the absence doing it.

Then at about second eight the lane arrives, a beat behind her phrase, and the
contradiction resolves into the thing the film is actually about: it works, it was
always working, and it was waiting.

The difference from card A matters and is worth stating plainly. A holds on an empty
lens with nothing happening, so a viewer cannot tell a deliberate absence from a
device that has not booted. D holds on an empty lens with a human working hard inside
the same frame, so the absence is legible as a choice from the first second, and then
the wake proves the device is alive before anybody can decide it is broken.

**How it runs across the three wearers.** The binding device is **the refusal**, and
it escalates. Three times in the film, at three crafts, Compass reaches the edge of
making the thing and stops:

1. **The musician.** The lane runs and the key lights at the moment to press, and
   she presses every key herself. Nothing sounds that she did not play.
2. **The photographer.** The checklist ticks as she gets each shot, and Compass never
   writes a line of it, because the list came from the client's brief. It is tracking,
   not directing.
3. **The illustrator.** The reference sits beside her line, never on it. Compass shows
   her the proportion it can see and never marks her drawing wrong.

Each refusal is a bigger ask than the last, and the third is the one the whole
replacement fear actually lives inside, which is why the illustrator goes last. The
villain is present in all three scenes as the thing that almost happened and did not.

**What it does with the one rule.** It treats the one rule as the film's subject
rather than as its premise. The other three cards obey the rule. This one is about the
rule. It is also the only card that carries the second half of the rule, the "it only
assists" half, because a film that shows Compass waking is only showing you half a
promise until it shows you where Compass stops.

And it is the only card that directly rebuilds the argument that died on 16 September.
The product definition's own steer is that the replacement for the Even Realities
contrast has to rest on **when Compass appears and what it refuses to do**, rather than
on how little it draws. Card A does the "when it appears" half. This card does both
halves, and it does the "refuses to do" half as its spine.

## 2. Distinctness check

| Card | What makes it different, in one line |
|---|---|
| **A · Nothing happens** | opens on an absence and binds on the wake; the film runs empty to full and its subject is arrival |
| **B · Three hands** | opens on work with the product hidden and binds on a hand-to-hand cut grammar; the film's structure is an edit rather than an event |
| **C · The stop** | opens on the lens at full stretch and binds on a simultaneous ending; the film runs full to empty and its subject is departure |
| **D · What it won't do** | opens on a contradiction, a working human and an idle machine in one frame, and binds on three escalating refusals; the film's subject is the boundary rather than the wake |

**Do any two collapse?** A and D sit closest, because both open third person on a wearer
with an empty lens. They are not the same film and here is the test that separates them:
take the interface out of both and ask what is left. A becomes a film about waiting, and
its structure disappears entirely, because without the wake there is no rhyme between the
three scenes. D becomes a film about three people whose machine keeps declining to help
more than it is asked to, and the structure survives, because the refusals are events that
happen whether or not you can see the graphics. They also run different engines: A's engine
is arrival and its emotional move is relief, D's engine is restraint and its emotional move
is trust. And they carry the villain differently, which is the thing step 6 will actually
have to choose between - A has the weakest answer to FP-21 in the set and D has the
strongest.

C and D both centre on restraint, but C's restraint is about time, in that Compass leaves
when you stop, and D's restraint is about the boundary, in that Compass never crosses into
making. Different films, and they would be storyboarded completely differently.

## 6. What the set does not cover, and what the truth sheet stopped

### Directions considered and not built

- **A statistics-led open.** The record already plans a small statistics passage, and in a
  silent film with a `problem-aware` viewer, type on screen is the most efficient way to make
  that viewer feel recognised in the first three seconds. It is also the class of open
  SP-HOOK-04 explicitly allows, because a number that sizes the PROBLEM is a different class
  from a market-opportunity number and is permitted to open. **Blocked by the truth sheet,
  and this is the biggest single thing it stopped.** Section 4 is full of live problems: the
  65 per cent has the wrong base written next to it and the subgroup's own size is not

  are being used in a creative-sector argument (B2), seven citations cannot currently be
  stood up at all (C1), and the honesty line says every number appears at the strength its
  source supports with its real base named. A film has even less room for a footnote than a
  poster does. So a stats open is not available until section 4 is CONFIRMED, and I did not
  build a concept on it.
- **A concept built on the proportion corrections.** The single most dramatic assistance beat
  available, because it is the one moment where Compass visibly knows something the maker does
  not. **Blocked by truth row A4**, which is unruled, and by the guardrail that the film may
  never write a beat that wins the replacement argument. Until A4 is ruled the way the sheet
  recommends, a correction reads as a machine marking your work wrong, which is the one thing
  this film cannot show. Card D's refusal three is as close as the set gets, and it only exists
  if A4 goes the recommended way.
- **A concept bound by privacy.** Three people, three private records of how they work, nothing
  leaving three rooms. It is the project's second real differentiator, it carries the Te Tiriti
  section of the report, and it is the thing a marker would most reward. **It has no image.**
  RS-CORE-03 is the rule that kept it out: held is not used, and a claim with no shot costs a
  segment and buys a title card. It lives in the press kit, the landing page and the report, as
  the brief already ruled.
- **A concept bound by the temple timing pulse.** The same pulse felt by three people in three
  rooms is a genuinely good binding device, and it would have bound the film physically rather
  than visually, which nothing else here does. **Retired on 16 September** (truth row A8), and
  the right call is not to restore a feature to save a concept.
- **A concept where Compass generates the shot list** and the photographer works to it. It
  would be a genuinely different and more provocative film, because the machine would be setting
  the agenda. It breaks the one rule, which is the hill this project will die on, and truth row
  A5 is the unruled version of exactly that question. Dead on the rule, not on the row.
- **Anything with a struggle beat, a failure beat or Compass speaking.** All ruled out by the
  register and by the never-do list, and I did not test them.

## 8. The director's read

Step 5's own skill says not to rank the cards or recommend one unless asked. This was asked
for, so it is here, clearly marked as a reading rather than a lock, and step 6 and the override
are still Polo's.

**I would carry D, What it won't do.**

The reason is not that it is the prettiest, because B is. It is that D is the only one of the
four that solves the problem the rest of this project is actually stuck on. Two of the arguments
that separated Compass from a heads-up display died on 16 September, the product definition says
so itself, and the replacement it gestures at is that the separation rests on **when Compass
appears and what it refuses to do**. Card A shows you when it appears. Card C shows you when it
leaves. Only D shows you both halves, and it makes the refusing half the spine, so the film ends
up being the argument rather than illustrating it.

It is also the only card whose hook is an active, founder-ruled pattern with the right axis fit
rather than a candidate or a gap, and the paradox it opens on, a machine switched on and idle
while a human works, is the single image in this whole set that could not be mistaken for any
other product in the category. That answers VG-AWARE-04 in the first second rather than at second
fifty, which matters more than usual here because the ruled placement is an exhibition screen and
AL-ALL-03 says that screen gives one glance.

And it is the card that most nearly resolves the brief's own item 2, the villain that never
appears. FP-14 wants the villain to be the spine and FP-21 wants the negative made real, and the
no-struggle ruling forbids staging either. Three refusals give you the villain in every scene
without a single struggle beat, because the thing that almost happened is the villain, and the
thing that did not happen is the product.

**What it costs to carry it.** Two things, both real. It is at or over the eight-shot compositing
cap, so item 6 either moves or a refusal gets cut, and cutting one takes the structure from three
legs to two. And it is the most truth-blocked of the four, because refusals two and three are
exactly rows A4, A5 and A6, so it cannot be storyboarded until those are ruled. Both of those are
rulings Polo can make in ten minutes and the truth sheet already recommends all three the way this
card needs.

**If he will not rule A4, A5 and A6, carry A instead.** A is the pack's own recommendation, it is
the most on-truth of the four against FP-23, and it has the most compositing headroom, and its
main weakness, that eleven seconds of nothing dies on an exhibition screen, is fixable without
touching the concept by cutting the 15-second loop out of the middle of the film rather than off
the front.

**C is the one I would keep on the table rather than cut**, because its payoff, three lenses
emptying on one frame, is the most retellable image anybody in this project has proposed, and it
would fold into D as D's ending without much trouble. That is the CP-LAUNCH-04 and pass-1 move:
one recommended direction plus one variant of it that is foldable into the film.

**B I would cut**, and say why in the report rather than quietly, because "the binding device is
a match cut" is technique wearing a concept's clothes, and a report graded on process is exactly
where that gets noticed.
